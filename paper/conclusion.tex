\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a pass on a tagged assessment item requires the tagged competency, and tested it without students by searching 2{,}405 public-release mathematics items for assessment bypasses under a pre-registered bar. Language models denied every given quantity recover the New York Regents Algebra~I and Algebra~II keys in two model families, and the recovery survives a form-preserving change of every option value. Catalog rules denied the tagged operation match keys on two cells at sizes that make them replication candidates. The one strategy program that survived Holm on its discovery cell did not replicate on held-out administrations. The TIMSS cognitive-domain cells yield no bypass at their public sizes. Every claimed rate is measured on items re-read from the printed page, which the released text layer often misstates.

On the items a bypass matches, a correct answer is read as evidence of an operation on quantities the solver never saw, so the key does not carry the evidence it is read as. That is a fact about the item and its scoring rule, and it can be checked before any student sits the test.

The search needs the tag text, the item text, the keys, an executable scoring rule, and a machine-enforceable restriction on the solver, by instruction set or by withholding part of the item; where those are public, it runs as it did here. Where they are not, the same search waits on a published scoring rule and a restriction a machine can enforce, and the census here is a lower bound on what that search finds. The one-sided scope of the claim is stated once with the bypass definition (Section~\ref{sec:method}); it is not repeated here.
