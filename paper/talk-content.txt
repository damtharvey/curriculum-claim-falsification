Does the Assessment Require the Skill?
A pass is read as evidence of its tag; a passing solver denied the tagged competency refutes that reading without students; Regents algebra has them.
one-sided: never says students did not learn


1. What's the big deal?
Every item carries a tag: a standard code, a cognitive domain, a rubric line.
A correct answer is read as evidence of the tagged competency. [Kane 2013]
That reading assumes the pass requires the competency. [AERA, APA, NCME 2014]
We can check that requirement with no students at all.
[Accountability, procurement, and research all make this reading; nobody checks the assumption before the test is given.]


2. What is new?
The assessment bypass: a solver denied the tagged competency that still passes under the published scoring rule.
Denied by operation (a program that cannot do the tagged step) or by information (the given quantities withheld).
Never sees the key. Executable. Student-free.
One-sided: a bypass refutes the requirement; finding none certifies nothing.
Pre-registered bar: at least 10 items, lower 95% confidence bound above chance, programs hashed before scoring.


3. What have others tried, and how is ours different?
Validity theory states the argument; it does not execute it. [Messick 1995; Kane 1992; Kane 2013]
Cognitive diagnosis and differential item functioning: need student responses. [Terzi and Sen 2019; Holland and Thayer 1986]
Test-wiseness catalogs: list cues, score no pass rates. [Millman et al. 1965; Haladyna et al. 2002; Attali and Bar-Hillel 2003]
Choices-only and contamination studies: models on untagged benchmarks. [Kaushik and Lipton 2018; Balepur et al. 2024; Sainz et al. 2023]
Ours: a restricted solver scored against a tag on public items. Nobody had.


4. How did we do it?
2,405 public-release mathematics items, nine authorities, every tag quoted from its authority. [IEA 2009 TIMSS 2011 frameworks]
Three solver families, each Holm-corrected on its own: item-writing cues, test-taking strategies, language models with every given quantity withheld.
Items re-read from the printed page: released text layers often misstate the print.
Isomorph control: change every option value, keep its form, ask if the rate survives. [Mirzadeh et al. 2025]
[Public keys and scoring rules make the whole search executable; that is why mathematics went first.]


5. What did we find?
New York Regents Algebra I and II: two model families recover the key with every given quantity hidden.
The recovery survives changing every option value.
So a pass there does not require computing on the givens; the key does not carry the evidence it is read as.
Small programs denied the tagged operation match keys on a few cells; one strategy discovery failed a powered replication.
Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) cognitive domains: no bypass at public sizes.
[Algebra I rate 0.49 to 0.55, Algebra II 0.41 to 0.45, chance 0.25. Under a stricter page check across two transcription model families, Algebra I holds on all three scorers and Algebra II on the two Qwen scorers only; Phi-4 misses there at n = 57. Whether the tagged operation is required stays open.]


6. So what?
Procurement, accountability, and research outcome measures read the wrong quantity when a tag overstates.
The check runs before any student sits the test.
It needs only public tags, item text, keys, and an executable scoring rule.


7. What does it imply for the education community?
Publish items with keys and scoring rules in machine-checkable form.
Treat a tag as a claim to be tested, not a label to be trusted. [AERA, APA, NCME 2014]
Item writers can run bypass regression tests before release.
The claim stays one-sided: about items and scoring rules, never about students.


8. What did we not show?
One subject: mathematics.
Item demand coded by language models, not by human raters.
Whether the tagged operation is required: left open.


9. Closing
A tag is a claim about what passing requires; where the inputs are public, it can be tested before anyone sits the test.
