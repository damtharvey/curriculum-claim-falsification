Released mathematics items carry a published tag: a curriculum
standard, or a cognitive domain such as reasoning. A correct answer is
then read as evidence of that competency in accountability, curriculum
evaluation, and research. If a pass does not require the tagged
operation, those uses are reading the wrong quantity. We ask whether a
program that lacks that operation can still match the published key. A
match is one-sided: that pass does not require the tagged operation, and
it does not say students failed to learn. Failure to find a program
does not certify the tag. Students are not measured.

On public-release items we score three families, each Holm-corrected (a
family-wise multiple-testing adjustment) on its own: catalog
item-writing rules, adaptive strategies, and partial-input language
models. A catalog of item-writing rules does not survive that correction.
Two catalog tests beat chance and remain candidates for replication, not
family-wise discoveries. Image-channel backsolving (transcribe the
printed equation, then substitute options) fires on a small
unique-equation subset of Algebra~I. A language model recovers Algebra~I
and Algebra~II keys with the given quantities withheld; on those items a
pass may require recognizing the form the wording names rather than
executing the tagged computation on the givens. The same search finds no
catalog, strategy, or partial-input program on Trends in International
Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) knowing, applying, or reasoning
items.
