Released mathematics items carry a published tag: a curriculum standard, or a cognitive domain such as reasoning. A correct answer is then read as evidence of that competency. We ask whether matching the published key requires the tagged operation, or whether a program that lacks that operation still matches the key. On 2{,}405 public-release items we score three families, each Holm-corrected on its own: catalog item-writing rules, hashed-then-scored adaptive strategies, and a partial-input language-model channel. Over 105 a priori catalog tests, five rows clear the pre-registered per-cell bar and none survives Holm correction. Two of those rows remain per-cell witnesses, meaning a program that lacks the tagged operation still matches the key: a signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue, lower-central, matches 90 of 279 New York Regents geometry keys ($0.323$, $n=279$, 95\% CI $[0.265, 0.380]$) and 20 of 40 Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) grade~6 keys ($0.500$, $n=40$, CI $[0.350, 0.650]$). On those matched items, a pass does not require the tagged operation. Arithmetic closure on State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR) grade~5 matches 21 of 40 keys ($0.525$, $n=40$, CI $[0.375, 0.675]$) and is the only Holm reject among 67 strategy tests; that discovery failed a powered replication on later-fetched administrations (combined $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$, which covers chance). Over 68 withheld-quantity masked-stem tests, a reader exists across two model families, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, and Phi-4, that recovers the key from question type plus option form on Regents Algebra~I and Algebra~II items whose given quantities are replaced by placeholders, strongest on expression and text options, with Phi-4 also clearing chance on numeric options ($0.366$, $n=71$) and clearing the same channel bar on geometry ($67/166=0.404$, CI $[0.331, 0.476]$ versus chance $0.250$ and modal letter $0.289$). Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 is a weak solver on the same items and does not clear the same bar, even when the original stem is restored. On those items a pass does not require executing the tagged computation on the givens; it may require recognizing the form the wording names. The same search recovers documented shallow-cue rates on OpenBookQA ($0.450$ versus a published $0.496$ question-ignored detector) and on SWAG ($0.565$ versus a published $0.436$ ending-only detector). It finds no catalog, strategy, or partial-input program on Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) items tagged knowing, applying, or reasoning, at a clustered bar that would have seen a bypass of $0.400$ to $0.421$. Accountability and research that treat a pass on those matched items as evidence of the tagged skill are then reading the wrong quantity.
