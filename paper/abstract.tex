Released mathematics items carry a published tag: a curriculum standard, or a cognitive domain such as reasoning. A correct answer is then read as evidence of that competency in accountability, curriculum evaluation, and research. If a pass does not require the tagged operation, those uses are reading the wrong quantity. We ask whether a program that lacks that operation can still match the published key. A match is one-sided: that pass does not require the tagged operation. Failure to find a program does not certify the tag. Students are not measured. On 2{,}405 public-release items we score three families, each Holm-corrected (a family-wise multiple-testing adjustment) on its own: catalog item-writing rules, adaptive strategies, and a partial-input language-model channel. Over 105 a priori catalog tests, none survives Holm. Two rows remain existence rows: they beat chance in that cell and are candidates for replication, not family-wise discoveries. Lower-central, a signed numeric-rank cue that picks the second-smallest option, matches 90 of 279 New York Regents geometry keys ($0.323$, $n=279$, item 95\% CI $[0.265, 0.380]$) and 20 of 40 Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) grade~6 keys ($0.500$, $n=40$, CI~$[0.350, 0.650]$), the latter only at that second-smallest option. Arithmetic closure on State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR) grade~5 is the only Holm reject among 67 strategy tests ($21/40=0.525$) and failed a powered replication (combined~$21/64=0.328$, CI~$[0.219, 0.438]$, which covers chance). Image-channel backsolving (transcribe the printed equation from the page, then substitute options) matches 14 of 15 rare Algebra~I items whose printed equation is satisfied by exactly one option ($n=15$, coverage $15/598$): not a Holm test and not a census of Algebra~I. Choices-only language models already recover keys without the question on several benchmarks. This census scores that family on tagged mathematics with masked-stem inputs (given quantities replaced by placeholders). Over 68 such tests, a reader exists in two model families, Qwen2.5 and Phi-4, on Regents Algebra~I ($n=358$) and Algebra~II ($n=248$). Geometry on Phi-4 is not claimed ($0.229$ on $n=118$ items whose key is not the cell's most common letter). Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 does not clear the same bar. One rater on a 40-item Algebra~I sheet scored~$15/40$, an uninformative null. On those Algebra~I and Algebra~II items a pass does not require executing the tagged computation on the givens; it may require recognizing the form the wording names. The same search recovers documented shallow-cue rates on OpenBookQA and SWAG. It finds no catalog, strategy, or partial-input program on Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) knowing, applying, or reasoning items at selected-response $n$ of about 50 to 80.
