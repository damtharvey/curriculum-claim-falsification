\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The instrument that reads the page is a vision-language model, and 175 of the 606 algebra items did not verify under two readings by Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct. A third reading by a different family, LLaVA-NeXT Vicuna 13B, agrees with the first Qwen reading on 160 items, 153 of them in the claimed population, so on that subset a reading error would have to be shared by two model families rather than made twice by one; on it the Algebra~I rows hold on all three scorers and the Algebra~II rows on the two Qwen scorers, and the Algebra~II Phi-4 row does not clear at $n=57$. What remains: 446 items do not verify across families, 5 of them two-page crops that exceeded the LLaVA context, so the claimed population is still mostly items two readings of one model agree on; only 20 items were checked against the page by a human; a hard-to-read item is more likely to be outside every verified set; and the two catalog rows rest on a single reading of their options. A human transcription of the 175 items that fail the Qwen check would let every row be reported on the full 606.

What an Algebra~I item asks is measured by two language-model coders and by the publisher's tag, and the three disagree: the coders agree with each other at $\kappa=0.576$ and with the cluster split at $0.071$ and $0.230$. No human coded the items, so a disagreement between coder and tag can be a coder error as easily as a tag error; automated tagging of item demand has that record elsewhere, where language-model Q-matrices for a reading test agreed with a validated expert Q-matrix at $\kappa$ up to $0.63$, with wide variation across models~\cite{Fan_Bialo_2026}. This limits the stronger statement: whether a pass on items that require operating on the givens omits that operation rests on the both-coders execution subset ($n=142$ verified), on which Phi-4 does not clear, and the subset itself moves with the coder. A human coding of the 358 Algebra~I items by two trained raters under the same rubric, with agreement reported, would fix the classes and is the measurement that would settle the question.

The template-memorization explanation is untested. The option-numeral control rules out recall of specific values, and the flat administration-year probe speaks against a template that drifted, but a reader that had learned the recurring stem-and-option templates of Regents items would keep its rate under a change of values exactly as the form component does. That leaves open whether the form component is a property of the item form or of the training corpus. The test is novel items written to the same templates that no model has seen, in the manner of a benchmark rewritten in the style of a public one~\cite{zhang2024gsm1k}, scored under the same masking.

The Qwen 14B text-layer isomorph arms are below the reproduction floor. Re-scored original letters agreed with the saved letters on $592/606=0.977$ against a $0.98$ floor, twice, and the cause is a logit shift between the torch version of the saved run and the torch version of both re-runs, which agree with each other to the last bit: six of the 14 changed items are exact ties on the re-run, an fp32 logits head recovers four, and one item moves by several nats (Appendix~\ref{app:repair}). The arms remain excluded and are shown as audit trail; the repaired-layer control covers Qwen 14B from a single model load and is the control we claim, so the gap affects only that trail. A pre-registered margin-based floor, or a re-run of the text-layer arms from one load, would close it.

The human masked-stem sheet has two raters on the same 40 items, and they split: the registered primary rater did not clear the bar ($15/40$) and the replication rater did ($21/40$), with letter agreement $\kappa=0.31$ between them. Whether humans in general read the same form cue as the models is therefore open, and the language-model finding is stated as a fact about models. Several raters on a sheet sized for the model rate against chance would settle it.

The EQAO grade~6 and geometry lower-central rows are small and unreplicated. EQAO grade~6 fires on 38 page-read items from three administrations; geometry fires on 246 items, and on the text layer 19 of 27 administrations are above chance but only one clears on its own; neither survives Holm over the 105 a priori tests. Both are existence rows, and a reader should hold them as hypotheses about the second-smallest option rather than as census findings. A pre-registered replication on administrations outside the census, EQAO releases after 2023 and Regents geometry forms not yet ingested, is the test.

The STAAR closure replication is limited to the forms we could fetch and to a parse we checked by hand on 20 items. The Holm-surviving discovery row replicated as an underpowered miss on 44 held-out grade~5 items and failed at $n=64$ once the 2017 and 2018 English forms were added; the 2015 key and the 2020 and 2023 tests were not found, the parse repair's hand check disagreed on 4 of 20, and 13 of the 40 discovery stems carry artifact numerals. The powered null therefore rests on those forms and that parse. Fetching the missing forms and hand-verifying the parse on all 64 replication items would make the null a clean one.

The instruction-only family, a retriever of worked examples from the same unit with no rule induction, is specified in the protocol and was not run, because curriculum scoring keys were not in the public files we could ingest. That restriction kind, the template imitator, is therefore untested, and the census tests three of the four specified families. An open curriculum whose unit assessments and keys are public would let it run.

Constructed-response items are scored only where a scoring guide reduces to a value. The 160 numeric-answer items were scored by the unbound-formula rules alone, explanations were never graded, and the constructed-response parts of each Regents administration were not ingested, so every claimed row is a fact about the selected-response part of the exam. An executor of the published rating guide, or value matching on the numeric-answer parts as a first channel, would extend the search to the rest of the form.

The ingestion is incomplete. Most NYSED 2018 graphic stems and the TIMSS 2007 release are out of this file, and earlier English STAAR forms entered only as a replication set. Those gaps lower the cell sizes, and with them the TIMSS power floors of Table~\ref{tab:power}; ingesting them is mechanical.
