\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We did not measure students. A witness is a fact about a key and a program. It is not a claim that students use the program.

The one-sided claim covers only the matched subset of each claimed cell: the 8 of 14 print-faithful EQAO grade~6 items on which the lower-central rule returns the published key; the print-faithful Algebra~I items on which the masked-stem model returns the key; and the 14 of 15 Algebra~I displayed-equation items on which unique substitution returns the key. A pass on an item the program does not match has not been shown to omit the tagged operation. The New York Regents geometry lower-central row ($90/279$ on the text layer) is not claimed: on the 99 print-faithful items its rate is unchanged and its interval covers chance.

We did not exhaust the class of programs that lack the tagged operation. A later search can find a TIMSS witness this one missed. On the withheld-quantity population, no channel in this file clears TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning. Public selected-response $n$ is about 50 to 80, and the clustered 80\% floors are $0.415$ to $0.441$, so a bypass rate in the low thirties would not have been seen. That null is power-limited and is not a certification of the tags.

Instruction-only (worked examples from the same unit) is specified in the protocol and was not run: curriculum scoring keys were not in the public files we could ingest.

About half of the census text layer in the claimed cells is not what students saw: of 955 items, 434 are print-faithful, 502 are not, and 19 could not be verified. Claims are made on the faithful subset. That subset is not a random sample of any cell; it over-represents administrations before 2023 (later Regents PDFs encode operators as digits) and, in Algebra~I, items with word options (40 percent of faithful items against 10 percent of not-faithful items). The faithful-subset rates say whether the effect exists on items whose text matches the print; they do not estimate the rate on the full population. The check itself errs in one direction: a transcription mistake by the vision-language model (a dropped digit in a set, a dropped $g(x)=$ prefix, accessibility alt text in EQAO forms) moves an item out of the faithful subset, never into it. Calibration against the 20-item hand audit agrees on 17 of 20; the 20-item audit itself is 9 matches, 8 glue cases, 2 truncations, and 1 wrong option set once a preview artifact in the audit tool is corrected. A witness on a corrupted option set does not refute the printed tag; a row that clears only on the text layer is not claimed.

The EQAO grade~6 catalog witness is specific to the pre-registered lower-central index, clears on 14 print-faithful items with interval $[0.286, 0.857]$, and does not clear under a post-hoc stricter fidelity filter ($5/10$). Upper-central on that cell is chance. It is a replication candidate.

STAAR grade~5 closure on the frozen 2019/2021/2022 cell is a discovery-set Holm reject ($21/40=0.525$) that did not replicate on held-out 2013/2014/2016/2017/2018 administrations after a pre-registered option-leak repair (combined $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$, $n\ge 46$). Thirteen of 40 discovery stems contain artifact numerals. The artifact-free discovery rate $16/27$ is on a population defined after the fact.

Text-layer backsolving does not reach $n\ge 10$ fired on any cell. The image addendum measures it on 15 of 598 Algebra~I solving-tagged items (coverage $0.025$); that is not a census of Algebra~I. The row is not a 67-family Holm test.

The language-model channel has the operations. What is withheld is information: the stem, or every given quantity. The inference is that the published key is determined by question type plus the option set, not that the model solved the tagged exercise and not that a student would match the key from that slice. The model is not a student. Items on which the masker withheld nothing are excluded from that claim; TIMSS knowing's first-run rate was partly those items. The Algebra~I witness holds on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, and Phi-4 on print-faithful items. Algebra~II cleared on the text layer and does not clear the modal-letter bar on print-faithful items on any of the three; it is not claimed. The standards split shows that the Algebra~I row is carried by items whose publisher's tag names recognizing or interpreting a form; on execution-tagged, print-faithful items ($n=75$) no model clears. The split classifies the text of the cluster heading, not each item's demand: a cluster such as ``understand solving equations as a process of reasoning'' sits over items that ask the solver to solve, and the split cannot see that. An item-level coding of the tags was not done. Geometry on Phi-4 meets the cell bar ($0.404$, $n=166$, lower CI above chance and above modal B $0.289$) but not the non-modal-key split ($0.229$, $n=118$) and is not claimed; it does not hold on Qwen or Mistral at the same bar. None of those cells hold on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3. Mistral's with-stem ceiling matches its masked-stem rate, so that miss is a weak solver. Qwen 7B also clears TEKS grade~5 and grade~8 at the registered channel bar on that scorer only; Qwen 14B and Phi-4 do not, so those rows are not claimed. On Qwen 14B the Algebra~I leftover is strongest on expression and text options and is weak on numeric options; Phi-4 also clears chance on numeric options ($0.366$, $n=71$).

The option-numeral control (Table~\ref{tab:isomorph}) leaves a form component in both scored models and also shows a numeral-specific component in Qwen 7B: on the 83 recognition-tagged faithful items its rate falls from $0.530$ to $0.361$ when every option value is changed with the form kept, and the row no longer clears the modal-letter bar. The control cannot say whether that component is memorized option values or value cues that survive the masking, such as an option whose magnitude fits the question type; both change under the substitution. Qwen 14B is not covered: its re-scored original letters reproduced the saved letters on $0.977$ of 606 items, below the pre-registered floor of $0.98$, so its perturbed arms were not scored and its Algebra~I row rests on the earlier probes alone. The earlier probes and this control together do not support memorization of the items as the whole effect; they do not rule out a memorized share.

We did not ingest every public paper. English forms from 2019, 2021, and 2022 are the discovery STAAR census. Earlier English STAAR forms scored here are a replication set, not additional census years. Most NYSED 2018 graphic stems, TIMSS 2007, and later restricted TIMSS cycles are out of this file.

Constructed-response items are not headline cells. Scoring guides that reduce to a value are matching; explanations were not graded here.
