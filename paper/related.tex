\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Standards labeling matches item text to a code. MathFish and related classifiers ask whether a labeler agrees with a publisher~\cite{lucy2024mathfish}. Agreement between labelers is not a test of whether the tagged operation is required to pass. We keep the publisher's tag and search for programs that pass without it.

Shortcut learning and partial-input baselines~\cite{Geirhos_2020,gururangan-etal-2018-annotation,poliak-etal-2018-hypothesis,mccoy-etal-2019-right,kaushik-lipton-2018-much} show that models and rules can answer from the wrong slice of the input. Visual question answering (VQA) models can keep the same answer across images~\cite{agrawal-etal-2016-analyzing}. Choices-only language models already beat majority on several multiple-choice benchmarks; that success can be a surface artifact or abductive reconstruction of the question~\cite{balepur-etal-2024-artifacts}. This census is that construction on tagged public mathematics items, not the first choices-only result: the scorer sees the option set, and in the masked condition the question type, but not the given quantities, and we Holm-correct within a census that includes TIMSS cells. Later papers in the same line separate ranking artifacts from question inference and survey how multiple-choice evaluation is scored~\cite{balepur-rudinger-2024-large,balepur-etal-2025-best}. A domain-naive classifier over stem and option features (length, stem to option and option to option similarity, keyword cues) trained to pick the keys of released science items reaches human-level guessing, and its above-chance accuracy is read as evidence that the item is a weak measure of the domain~\cite{Watson_2018}; that is the closest program prior to our catalog and strategy families, which differ in using a priori hashed rules rather than a fitted classifier, tagged public mathematics, Holm correction within each family, and no student reference. Contamination of released items in training data is a rival explanation of those hits~\cite{magar-schwartz-2022-data,sainz-etal-2023-nlp}; the Algebra~I and Algebra~II readers we claim have negative contamination probes and keep their rates above the channel bar when every option value is changed with the form kept (Section~\ref{sec:partial-input}). Substituting the numerals in grade-school word problems lowers language-model accuracy~\cite{mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic}; that is the complementary experiment to withholding the given quantities, and we run that substitution on the option strings of the masked-stem Algebra~I and Algebra~II items as a control on the form reading. Item-writing and test-wiseness catalogs~\cite{Millman_1965,Haladyna_2002,Haladyna_Rodriguez_2013} list surface cues that can determine a choice without content, including using the numbers given without binding them. Those guidelines have been compiled into automatic item-writing-flaw detectors (longest option, absolute terms, convergence, repeated words)~\cite{Moore_2024}; that toolkit flags flaws rather than scoring pass rates, and the four extra catalog cues we scored after primary scoring overlap it. Some option sets already contain information about which member is the key even with the stem withheld~\cite{White_Zammarelli_1981}. Arithmetic closure is that unbound-execution move as a program. Those catalogs are our channels, not claims about students. Test makers and takers hide and seek keys in middle letter positions~\cite{Attali_2003}; the lower-central program here picks a middle numeric rank, which is not that position cue. Plug-in substitution of numbered options is a documented SAT mathematics strategy~\cite{Katz_Bennett_Berger_2000}; the image-channel row is that strategy as a program on a displayed equation, not a student protocol.

Q-matrix and cognitive-diagnosis methods estimate which skills an item requires from student response data~\cite{Tatsuoka_1983,de_la_Torre_Chiu_2016}. Expert attribute tags on TIMSS 2011 items have been treated as misspecifiable Q-matrix entries under the generalized deterministic inputs, noisy ``and'' gate (G-DINA) model~\cite{Terzi_Sen_2019}. Differential item functioning (DIF) uses examinee groups to detect items that do not measure the same construct across populations~\cite{Holland_Thayer_1986}. Language models have also been scored on the released TIMSS 2011 grade~8 mathematics items with item response theory against human populations~\cite{fang-etal-2025-patch}; that study asks how well models solve the items, whereas this census asks whether a partial-input program matches keys without the tag, so our TIMSS language-model null on the clean-option subset should be read next to that full-item result. This census is the student-free adversarial counterpart: no response vector is used. Comparisons to percent correct, when we make them, use only the statistics TIMSS already published with the public-release items.

Validity, cognitive diagnosis, test-wiseness, choices-only evaluation, and contamination detection each already study one piece of what a tagged correct answer can be taken to mean. Argument-based validity treats a tagged pass as a claim that must be supported for the intended use~\cite{Kane_1992,Kane_2013,Messick_1995,aera2014standards}. Cognitive diagnosis and differential item functioning estimate attributes or bias from student responses. Test-wiseness catalogs list cues that can determine a choice without the tagged skill. Choices-only and contamination papers measure models on mostly untagged general-knowledge items. Domain-naive guessers, item-writing-flaw detectors, psychometric benchmarking of models on TIMSS items, and numeral-substitution tests each occupy one vertex of that space~\cite{Watson_2018,Moore_2024,fang-etal-2025-patch,mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic}. Taken together they still do not score this paper's object: a one-sided program witness on tagged public mathematics items, meaning a program that lacks the tagged operation, never sees the published key, and still matches under the published scoring rule. A match is evidence that a pass does not require the tag. Failure to find a program does not certify the tag. Each family of programs is Holm-corrected on its own. That object is not a claim about whether students learned.
