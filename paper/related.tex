\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Standards labeling matches item text to a code. MathFish and related classifiers ask whether a labeler agrees with a publisher~\cite{lucy2024mathfish}; agreement between labelers is not a test of whether the tagged operation is required to pass. We keep the publisher's tag and search for programs that pass without it.

Shortcut learning and partial-input baselines~\cite{Geirhos_2020,gururangan-etal-2018-annotation,poliak-etal-2018-hypothesis,mccoy-etal-2019-right,kaushik-lipton-2018-much} show that models and rules can answer from the wrong slice of the input, and visual question answering (VQA) models can keep the same answer across images~\cite{agrawal-etal-2016-analyzing}. Choices-only language models already beat majority on several multiple-choice benchmarks, by surface artifact or by abductive reconstruction of the question~\cite{balepur-etal-2024-artifacts}, and later work separates ranking artifacts from question inference and surveys how multiple-choice evaluation is scored~\cite{balepur-rudinger-2024-large,balepur-etal-2025-best}; our masked-stem condition is that construction on tagged mathematics items, with the question type shown and the given quantities withheld. A domain-naive classifier over stem and option features trained to pick the keys of released science items reaches human-level guessing, read as evidence that the item is a weak measure of the domain~\cite{Watson_2018}; that is the closest program prior to our catalog and strategy families, which use a priori hashed rules rather than a fitted classifier. Contamination of released items in training data is a rival explanation of partial-input hits~\cite{magar-schwartz-2022-data,sainz-etal-2023-nlp}, and substituting the numerals of grade-school word problems lowers language-model accuracy~\cite{mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic}; our option-numeral control is that substitution applied to the option strings. Item-writing and test-wiseness catalogs~\cite{Millman_1965,Haladyna_2002,Haladyna_Rodriguez_2013} list surface cues that can determine a choice without content, including using the given numbers without binding them, and have been compiled into item-writing-flaw detectors that flag flaws rather than scoring pass rates~\cite{Moore_2024}; some option sets carry information about the key with the stem withheld~\cite{White_Zammarelli_1981}; test makers and takers hide and seek keys in middle letter positions~\cite{Attali_2003}, a position cue and not the numeric rank our lower-central rule picks; plug-in substitution of numbered options is a documented SAT mathematics strategy~\cite{Katz_Bennett_Berger_2000}, which our image channel runs as a program on the displayed equation. Those catalogs are our programs, not claims about students.

Q-matrix and cognitive-diagnosis methods estimate which skills an item requires from student response data~\cite{Tatsuoka_1983,de_la_Torre_Chiu_2016}, and expert attribute tags on TIMSS 2011 items have been treated as misspecifiable Q-matrix entries under the generalized deterministic inputs, noisy ``and'' gate (G-DINA) model~\cite{Terzi_Sen_2019}. Differential item functioning (DIF) uses examinee groups to detect items that do not measure the same construct across populations~\cite{Holland_Thayer_1986}. Language models have been scored on the released TIMSS 2011 grade~8 mathematics items with item response theory against human populations~\cite{fang-etal-2025-patch}; that study asks how well models solve the items, and our TIMSS language-model null should be read next to it. This census uses no response vector, and comparisons to percent correct use only the statistics TIMSS published with the items.

Each of those lines studies one piece of what a tagged correct answer can be taken to mean. Argument-based validity treats the tagged pass as a claim to be supported for its use~\cite{Kane_1992,Kane_2013,Messick_1995,aera2014standards}; cognitive diagnosis and DIF estimate attributes or bias from student responses; test-wiseness catalogs list cues; choices-only and contamination papers measure models on mostly untagged general-knowledge items; domain-naive guessers, flaw detectors, psychometric benchmarking of models on TIMSS items, and numeral-substitution tests each occupy one vertex of that space~\cite{Watson_2018,Moore_2024,fang-etal-2025-patch,mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic}. None scores an assessment bypass on tagged public mathematics items, and that object is what this paper adds.
