\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Claims and corpora}

Each claim is quoted from its authority. Utah Core grades 6--8 use Utah State Board of Education Core Guides. CCSS seed codes are checked against the same wording. TIMSS uses the 2011 framework definitions of knowing, applying, and reasoning~\cite{iea2009timss2011frameworks}.

Targets are released summative items and end-of-unit assessments with scoring rules. Practice problems are never targets. This census contains 2{,}405 keyed public items (2{,}245 selected-response, 160 numeric): New York Regents 1{,}448; State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR) 445; Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) 149; TIMSS 2011 129; earlier TIMSS public-release 106; Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 70; New York State Education Department (NYSED) 27; National Assessment Program Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN) 21; Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) 10. Operational Utah RISE items are secure and are not used. TIMSS 2015 and later are not used.

\subsection{Channels}

A channel fixes the information and the instruction set, and therefore the operations the program lacks. This census scores three families. The catalog family is the (rule, cell) list locked in the protocol draft; strategy programs were hashed before any pass rate was computed; the language-model family was scored the same day and Holm-corrected inside that family only. Hashes, timestamps, model revisions, seeds, and filter counts are in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\paragraph{Catalog rules.}
Item-writing cues (option length, position, absolute terms, and a signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue) lack every content operation. Stem--option overlap and stem-number copying are shallow partial-input rules. Unbound execution applies a named formula (percent of, unit rate, $y=kx$, and the others in the 15-rule catalog) to stem numerals without binding symbols to the situation; it lacks bind and explain. Instruction-only (worked examples from the same unit) is specified but was not run: curriculum scoring keys were not in the public files we could ingest.

\paragraph{Adaptive strategy programs.}
Five programs, written from the same catalogs, were hashed and then scored; version-1 rates were not overwritten after later parsing edits. A distractor-derivation hub picks the option from which the most other options can be derived under a fixed set of error transforms. Constraint elimination removes options that a shallow stem keyword rules out (a negative count, a probability outside $[0,1]$, a mean outside the listed data). Arithmetic closure applies $+,-,\times,\div$, squares, and square roots to the stem numbers to depth two and picks the option that first appears. Backsolving parses a one- or two-variable equation from the stem and picks the unique option that satisfies it. A fixed-order portfolio runs those four. None of the five binds a symbol to the situation the standard names. Chance for a rule that eliminates options is the mean of one divided by the number of remaining options, not $0.25$. Hub and portfolio must also exceed the 95th percentile of random programs of matched complexity. Cells are every authority-by-claim pair with at least 10 selected-response items. A (program, cell) row enters the family when $n$ scored is at least 10 and chance is positive: 67 tests. Holm is inside this family. A 172-test pool with the 105 catalog tests is a sensitivity, not a pool with the language-model tests. The catalog unbound formulas already scored in the 105 are a comparison, not this family's closure program. Text-layer backsolving enters that family only when a cell has $n\ge 10$ unique fires; it does not. A later addendum, hashed before scoring and not a member of those 67 tests, uses a vision-language model (VLM) to transcribe the displayed equation from a page image, then substitutes each option. The model is not asked to solve. Invented formulas and extracts that do not match the page are abstentions. The addendum bar is $n$ fired at least 10 and a lower 95\% interval strictly above chance. Tie-breaks, the transform set, and the frozen hashes are in Appendix~\ref{app:strategies}.

\paragraph{Partial-input language-model channel.}
A local instruction-tuned language model sees a slice of the item and picks the option letter with the highest next-token log-probability. There is no chain of thought and no free generation. Two model families, Qwen2.5 (7B-Instruct and 14B-Instruct) and Phi-4, are capable scorers; Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 is a weak-solver check. Two prompts: options only (stem withheld entirely) and masked stem plus options. Masking replaces every numeral, number word, fraction, percentage, decimal, and value binding in the stem with the placeholder $[N]$; question wording, units, operators, and the option strings are kept. Items on which the masker withheld nothing are excluded from the primary population, because the tagged operation may still be performable; the primary population is the 1{,}215 items with at least one withheld quantity. The witness bar for this channel is: $n$ at least 10, and the lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval strictly above both format chance and that cell's modal-letter frequency, on the withheld-quantity population. A pass on those items does not require processing the givens. Contamination probes, not extra family tests, are mean token log-probability of the original unmasked stem plus options, administration-year buckets, option-letter permutation, and a second masking pass that also hides named figure types, variable bindings, and leftover coefficients. Holm for this channel is every scored (model, cell) on the withheld-quantity masked-stem population with $n$ at least 10: 68 tests (four models, 17 cells). Options-only is a ladder rung, not a member of that family. Holm is inside this family only. Catalog and strategy stay 105 and 67; the 172-test programmatic pool is a sensitivity and is not mixed with these 68. Model revisions, the letter-token rule, the clean-option filter, and the masking-rule hashes are in Appendix~\ref{app:lm}.

\subsection{Admission and the witness bar}

Two kinds of catalog program are admitted.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A priori rules}, taken from the catalogs with a citation each and no fitted parameters, scored on every item they apply to. There are 15 such programs (option length, position, absolute terms, a signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue named below, stem--option overlap, stem-number copying, and unbound formulas).
\item \emph{Fitted or searched programs}, trained on items disjoint from those they are scored on. Headline form is cross-corpus: fit on state items, score on TIMSS, or the reverse. Reported accuracy is held-out only.
\end{enumerate}

The adaptive strategy programs and the partial-input language-model channel are separate families, pre-registered on the scoring day as above, not members of the 105. They do not replace the catalog family.

The catalogs list a numeric-central-value cue: among numeric options, the key is often a middle value~\cite{Millman_1965,Haladyna_2002}. The a priori program we score is a signed variant of that cue, which we call lower-central: parse a leading signed number from each option; if at least three parse, sort them and pick the option at index $\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor$. For four options that is the second-smallest number. The catalog median of four numbers is the average of the two central values, which is not an option, so a discrete program must pick one side. That index is fixed in the program; it is not a parameter fit on these pass rates. A protocol draft records option numeric rank (minimum, median, maximum) as searched features, not this exact a priori index. The same index is the named tie-break inside the strategy programs.

A catalog or strategy program is a witness on a claim only if $n$ scored is at least 10, the lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of the pass rate exceeds format chance (one divided by the number of options on that item, or the mean of one divided by remaining options after elimination; $0.25$ on four-option selected items with no elimination), and (for searched, fitted, hub, and portfolio programs) the pass rate is at or above the 95th percentile of random programs of matched complexity, which we call the machine bar. The partial-input language-model bar is stated in the paragraph above and is not loosened. A singleton match is logged, not claimed. Random-weight programs are the baseline, never witnesses.

The searched class is a library of surface-cue programs enumerated to bounded complexity, plus greedy and evolutionary search (Appendix~\ref{app:searched}). Fourteen selected on the training corpus are scored on the other corpus only.

\subsection{Controls}

Before a census null means anything, the same searcher has to find witnesses where they are known to exist. We run a synthetic Haladyna-flaw set (circular: the planted cues are the features) and, as the primary control, public multiple-choice corpora whose authors already published an options-only or question-ignored number~\cite{mihaylov-etal-2018-suit,zellers-etal-2018-swag,lai-etal-2017-race,talmor-etal-2019-commonsenseqa}.
