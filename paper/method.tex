\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The object}
\label{sec:object}

A cell is an authority-by-tag group of items with published keys, and a solver passes an item when it returns the key under the published scoring rule. An \emph{assessment bypass} is a solver policy that lacks the tagged competency by an explicit restriction written into its specification, never sees the key, and passes anyway. What the policy lacks is a fact about its specification, not about its internals, and the restriction is of one of two kinds. A \emph{capability restriction} removes an operation: item-writing cues, test-taking strategies, and substitution of the options into the printed equation lack the tagged operation because it is not in their instruction set, so a bypass by one of them refutes the claim that the pass requires that operation. An \emph{information restriction} removes an input: a language model shown the item with every given quantity withheld has the operations and lacks the inputs, so a bypass by it refutes the claim that the pass requires computing on those givens. It does not refute the claim that the pass requires the relation the tag names, which the model can apply to the form of the options without the numbers. Score use rests on the first claim: a correct answer is read as evidence that the student carried the operation out on the quantities in front of them, and a key that can be matched without those quantities does not carry that evidence. A family that finds no bypass on a cell leaves the part of the competency it was denied unrefuted there.

\subsection{Items and tags}
\label{sec:corpus}

Targets are released summative items and end-of-unit assessments with scoring rules; practice problems are never targets. The census holds 2{,}405 keyed public items (2{,}245 selected-response, 160 numeric): New York Regents 1{,}448; State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR) 445; Ontario's Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) 149; TIMSS 2011 129; earlier TIMSS public-release 106; Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 70; New York State Education Department (NYSED) 27; National Assessment Program Literacy and Numeracy (NAPLAN) 21; Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) 10. Each tag is quoted from its authority: Utah Core grades 6--8 use Utah State Board of Education Core Guides, CCSS seed codes are checked against the same wording, and TIMSS uses the 2011 framework definitions of knowing, applying, and reasoning~\cite{iea2009timss2011frameworks}. TIMSS items keep those tags; we do not assign CCSS codes to them. TIMSS 2011 is the last cycle whose item texts, keys, scoring guides, domain tags, and percent correct are public together with no permission process; from 2015 the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA) keeps most items secured, and restricted-use grants cap example items and forbid the comparisons this census needs, so later cycles are not used and recency of showable items comes from released state and provincial tests. Operational Utah RISE items are secure and are not used. A cell enters scoring when it has at least 10 selected-response items.

\subsection{Three families of solvers}
\label{sec:families}

A family fixes the information and the instruction set its programs receive, and therefore the restriction each carries. Each family was fixed before its pass rates were computed: the catalog family is the (rule, cell) list locked in the protocol draft, the strategy programs were hashed before scoring and later parsing edits did not overwrite version-1 rates, and the language-model family was scored the day it was registered. Hashes, timestamps, model revisions, seeds, and filter counts are in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\paragraph{Catalog rules (capability-restricted).}
Fifteen a priori programs taken from the item-writing and test-wiseness catalogs, each with a citation and no fitted parameter, scored on every item they apply to: option length, position, absolute terms, a signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue, stem--option overlap, stem-number copying, and unbound formulas (percent of, unit rate, $y=kx$, and the rest of the catalog) applied to stem numerals without binding a symbol to the situation. The catalogs state the central-value cue as: among numeric options, the key is often a middle value~\cite{Millman_1965,Haladyna_2002}. The variant we score, \emph{lower-central}, parses a leading signed number from each option and, if at least three parse, sorts them and picks the option at index $\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor$, the second-smallest of four. The catalog median of four numbers is the average of the two central values, which is not an option, so a discrete program must pick one side; that index is fixed in the program and not fit on these pass rates, and a protocol draft records option numeric rank as a searched feature rather than this exact index. The same index is the tie-break inside the strategy programs. A second class of catalog program is fitted or searched: a library of surface-cue programs enumerated to bounded complexity plus greedy and evolutionary search (1{,}847 programs, Appendix~\ref{app:searched}), fit on state items and scored on TIMSS or the reverse, with fourteen programs selected on the training corpus and held-out accuracy only. Instruction-only (worked examples from the same unit) is specified and was not run: curriculum scoring keys were not in the public files we could ingest.

\paragraph{Adaptive strategy programs (capability-restricted).}
Five programs written from the same catalogs. A distractor-derivation hub picks the option from which the most other options can be derived under a fixed set of error transforms. Constraint elimination removes options that a shallow stem keyword rules out (a negative count, a probability outside $[0,1]$, a mean outside the listed data). Arithmetic closure applies $+,-,\times,\div$, squares, and square roots to the stem numbers to depth two and picks the option that first appears. Backsolving parses a one- or two-variable equation from the stem and picks the unique option that satisfies it. A fixed-order portfolio runs those four. None binds a symbol to the situation the standard names. Text-layer backsolving enters the family only when a cell has at least 10 unique fires, which none does, so a later addendum, hashed before scoring and outside the family, has a vision-language model (VLM) transcribe the displayed equation from the page image and substitutes each option symbolically; the model is not asked to solve, and an invented formula or an extract that does not match the page is an abstention. Tie-breaks, the transform set, and the frozen hashes are in Appendix~\ref{app:strategies}.

\paragraph{Partial-input language models.}
Here the restriction is on information. A local instruction-tuned language model sees a slice of the item and picks the option letter with the highest next-token log-probability; there is no chain of thought and no free generation. Two model families, Qwen2.5 (7B-Instruct and 14B-Instruct) and Phi-4, are capable scorers; Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 is a weak-solver check. Two prompts are scored: options only, with the stem withheld entirely, and \emph{masked stem}, in which every numeral, number word, fraction, percentage, decimal, and value binding in the stem is replaced by the placeholder $[N]$ while question wording, units, operators, and the option strings are kept. Options only is an information-ladder rung; the masked stem is the bypass. A mechanical clean-option filter first drops items whose options carry exam footers, item codes, country-table fragments, options longer than 120 characters, or bleed from the next stem, keeping 1{,}496 of 2{,}245 selected-response items, and items on which the masker withheld nothing are excluded because the tagged operation may still be performable, leaving a primary population of 1{,}215 items with at least one withheld quantity. Model revisions, the letter-token rule, the filter, and the masking hashes are in Appendix~\ref{app:lm}.

Two kinds of probe attach to this family, and neither is a family test. Contamination probes are the mean token log-probability of the original unmasked stem plus options, administration-year buckets, option-letter permutation, and a second masking pass that also hides named figure types, variable bindings, and leftover coefficients. The pre-registered \emph{option-numeral isomorph control} rescores the same masked stems with every numeral in the option strings replaced by a value-changing, form-preserving isomorph (same digit count, sign, decimal places, and comma grouping; the same replacement wherever a literal repeats within an item; one arm preserves the rank order of the options' leading numbers and one scrambles it), with the key letter unchanged: a rate that holds is read from the form of the options, and a rate that falls to chance was read from their values (Appendix~\ref{app:lm}).

\subsection{The witness bar and the Holm families}
\label{sec:bar}

A program is a \emph{witness} on a cell only if it scores at least 10 items there and the lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of its pass rate exceeds format chance: one divided by the number of options, or, for a program that eliminates options, the mean of one divided by the options remaining, so $0.25$ on four-option items with no elimination. Searched, fitted, hub, and portfolio programs must also reach the 95th percentile of random programs of matched complexity, which we call the machine bar; random-weight programs are the baseline and never witnesses. The language-model bar adds the modal key letter: the lower bound must exceed both chance and the frequency of the most common key letter in that population, on the withheld-quantity items. A singleton match is logged, not claimed.

Multiplicity is controlled inside each family and never across them. The catalog family is every (rule, cell) pair with at least 10 scored items and positive chance, 105 tests. The strategy family is every (program, cell) pair at the same floor, 67 tests. The language-model family is every (model, cell) pair on the withheld-quantity masked-stem population with at least 10 items, 68 tests (four models, 17 cells); options only is not a member. Each family is Holm-corrected at $\alpha=0.05$ on its own, so a row that clears the per-cell bar in one cell is never billed as a family-wise discovery, and a 172-test pool of the catalog and strategy tests is reported as a sensitivity only. The image-backsolving addendum and the probes above are outside every family; the addendum's bar is at least 10 fires and a lower 95\% interval strictly above chance.

\subsection{Controls}
\label{sec:controls-method}

Before a census null means anything, the same searcher has to find witnesses where they are known to exist. We run a synthetic set with planted item-writing flaws (circular: the planted cues are the features) and, as the primary control, public multiple-choice corpora whose authors already published an options-only or question-ignored number~\cite{mihaylov-etal-2018-suit,zellers-etal-2018-swag,lai-etal-2017-race,talmor-etal-2019-commonsenseqa}.

\subsection{Print fidelity}
\label{sec:fidelity-method}

Every rate was first computed on the PDF text layer of the released form, and a 20-item hand audit against the printed pages found that layer wrong on about half of the sampled rows (Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}). Two further layers follow, and claims are made on the second.

\paragraph{Filter.}
For every item in the cells that cleared on the text layer (955 items: the two algebra masked-stem populations, the two lower-central fired sets, and the unclaimed Phi-4 geometry cell), a VLM (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, the model used for image backsolving) transcribes the stem and options from the rendered page image; it is never asked to solve or to pick a letter. Transcription and text layer are normalized identically and compared token by token: the stem must reach token F1, precision, and recall of at least $0.8$, every option must match at $0.9$, and the option counts must agree, and the item is then \emph{print-faithful}; an unparseable transcription is \emph{unverified} and is excluded from both subsets. Thresholds, prompt, and normalization were frozen and hashed before any population item was scored, after development on 40 items outside the population, and were calibrated on the 20 hand-audited items: 17 of 20 agree on the binary verdict, above the pre-registered floor of 16, so the thresholds were not adjusted. Every row that cleared on the text layer was recomputed on the faithful subset and on its complement from the saved per-item flags and predictions, with the same bootstraps and bars. The faithful subset is not a random sample of the cell: it over-represents administrations before 2023, whose PDFs do not encode operators as digits, and items with word options, and a transcription error moves an item out of it and never in (Appendix~\ref{app:fidelity}).

\paragraph{Repair.}
The filter keeps a selected subset; the repaired layer replaces the text layer for every item in the two algebra masked-stem populations (606 items). The transcription above becomes the item's stem and options, and a second transcription of the same page by the same model, with a reworded prompt and a larger crop, is compared to the first under the thresholds above. An item on which the two agree is \emph{repaired-verified}; every other item is \emph{repaired-unverified}. The key letter is always the census key. Every masked-stem, item-class, and isomorph row is then rescored on the repaired stems and options with the same models, seed, and letter rule. The procedure, prompt, thresholds, and the amendment that re-runs Qwen 14B on the isomorph arms were hashed before the second transcription and before any scoring on this layer (Appendix~\ref{app:repair}). Language-model claims are made on the repaired-verified population; the full repaired population is a sensitivity, and the text-layer and filtered rows are an audit trail (Appendix~\ref{app:trail}). The two catalog rows that cleared on the text layer are rerun on the stored first transcription of their options, without a second transcription.

\subsection{Two codings of what an item asks}
\label{sec:codings-method}

The language-model rows carry two codings of item demand. The first is the publisher's tag: the NYSED rating guide of every Regents administration maps each question to a CCSS cluster, such as \texttt{A-REI.B}, so every item in the two algebra populations has one. Under a pre-registered rule, the verbs of each cluster heading classify the cluster as \emph{execution} (solve, rewrite, factor, complete the square, create equations, compute, graph, perform arithmetic operations, and the like: operating on given quantities), \emph{recognition or interpretation} (identify, interpret, recognize, compare, explain, understand), or \emph{mixed} when the heading carries both; the rule and the classification of all 33 clusters were hashed before any prediction was joined to a tag (Appendix~\ref{app:standards}). That is a classification of the tag's text, not of the item. The second coding is item-level: two language-model coders, GLM 5.2 and Kimi K3, each read every Algebra~I item's stem and options, with the key, every prediction, and the cluster split withheld, and assigned one of the same three codes under one fixed rubric (Appendix~\ref{app:coders}); agreement between the coders and with the tag is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$. The masked-stem rows are recomputed on each tag class, on the items both coders give the same code, and on the intersection of those with the tag, at the unchanged bar. A statement about the tagged operation needs the items both coders mark as requiring operation on the givens; a statement about withheld information needs no such population.
