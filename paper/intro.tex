\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An assessment item arrives with a claim about what answering it requires. Utah Core and the Common Core State Standards (CCSS) print a standard code. The Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) prints knowing, applying, or reasoning~\cite{iea2009timss2011frameworks}. A passing score is then used as evidence of that competency: in accountability, in curriculum evaluation, and in research that treats tagged items as operationalizations of skills. If a pass does not require the tagged operation, those uses are reading the wrong quantity.

That inference is an argument, not a definition~\cite{Kane_1992,Messick_1995}. It can be wrong even when the publisher's tag is sincere. The item may be answerable from a surface cue, from the choices without the stem, or from a formula that never binds symbols to the situation the standard names.

This paper searches for those rival explanations as programs. A program that lacks an operation the tag names, never sees the published key, and still matches the key under the published scoring rule is a \emph{witness}. A witness refutes the claim that a pass on that item requires the tagged operation. A search that finds no witness does not certify the tag beyond the programs that were tried. We do not estimate whether students learned the skill, whether humans use the shortcuts, or which curriculum is better. We never say students do not learn~$X$.

The object is a requirement matrix $E[t,o]$: an item-by-operation table that is $0$ if some admitted program lacking $o$ still matches the published key of item $t$, and $1$ if none of the searched programs that lack $o$ do. Operations are shared across authorities so that tags can be compared: retrieve, execute, bind, distinguish, explain, transfer. TIMSS items keep knowing, applying, and reasoning. We do not assign CCSS codes to TIMSS items.

This census of 2{,}405 public-release items finds no family-wise catalog discovery, two per-cell catalog witnesses that hold under administration clustering, one strategy Holm reject on a frozen State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR) grade~5 cell that failed a powered replication, an existential partial-input reader across two model families on Regents Algebra~I and Algebra~II items whose given quantities are replaced by placeholders and on geometry for Phi-4, and a null on TIMSS knowing, applying, and reasoning at clustered floors of $0.400$ to $0.421$. Catalog, strategy, and language-model tests are Holm-corrected as three families.

\begin{itemize}
\item Over 105 a priori tests, five rows clear the per-cell bar and none survives Holm. Two remain per-cell witnesses, candidates for replication: lower-central, a signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue, matches 90 of 279 New York Regents geometry keys ($0.323$, $n=279$, clustered CI $[0.265, 0.374]$) and 20 of 40 Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) grade~6 keys ($0.500$, $n=40$, clustered CI $[0.417, 0.588]$). The EQAO witness is specific to that pre-registered index. On those matched items, a pass does not require the tagged operation.
\item Arithmetic closure on STAAR grade~5 matches 21 of 40 keys ($0.525$, $n=40$, CI $[0.375, 0.675]$) and is the only Holm reject among 67 hashed-then-scored strategy tests. That discovery failed a powered replication on later-fetched administrations (combined $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$). A 172-test pool with the catalog is a sensitivity with the same single Holm reject.
\item Over 68 masked-stem tests on items with at least one quantity replaced by a placeholder, a reader exists across two model families, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, and Phi-4, that recovers the key from question type plus option form on Regents Algebra~I and Algebra~II, strongest on expression and text options, with Phi-4 also clearing chance on numeric options ($0.366$, $n=71$) and clearing the same channel bar on geometry ($0.404$, $n=166$, CI $[0.331, 0.476]$ versus modal $0.289$). Contamination probes for those readers are negative. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 is a weak solver whose with-stem ceiling matches its masked-stem rate and does not clear the same bar, even when the original stem is restored. On those items a pass does not require executing the tagged computation on the givens; it may require recognizing the form the wording names.
\item No catalog, strategy, or partial-input program clears its channel bar on TIMSS items tagged knowing, applying, or reasoning. Those cells would have detected a clustered bypass of $0.400$, $0.407$, and $0.421$ (independent-item floors $0.358$, $0.353$, and $0.372$). The same 1{,}847-program search recovers published shallow-cue rates on OpenBookQA and SWAG, so that null is not a failure to detect planted or documented cues.
\end{itemize}

This census uses public-release items. TIMSS 2011 is the last cycle whose item texts, keys, scoring guides, domain tags, and percent correct are public together, with no permission process. From 2015, the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA) keeps most items secured; restricted-use grants typically cap example items and forbid the comparisons this census needs. Recency of showable items comes from released state and provincial tests, not from a later TIMSS cycle.
