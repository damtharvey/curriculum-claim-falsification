\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The text layer against the printed page}
\label{sec:print-audit}

A 20-item hand audit, 10 geometry lower-central hits and 10 Algebra~I masked-stem hits, compared each census row to the source exam PDF by eye. Nine rows match stem, options, and key. Eight have footer, figure-label, or previous-item glue on an option or stem. Two are truncated. One Algebra~I row has the printed stem and a different option set (June 2023 item 3: printed lines $y=2x-2$ and three neighbors; census inequalities). No sampled PDF was missing.

The mechanical filter of Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-method} then classified all 955 items in the cells that had cleared on the text layer: 434 print-faithful, 502 not faithful, 19 unverified. The text layer is wrong in two main ways: glue from footers, diagram labels, table cells, or the next item (241 items), and option sets or stems that do not match the printed item (230), most of them Regents forms from 2023 onward whose PDFs encode $=$, $+$, and $-$ as the digits 5, 1, and 2. The faithful fraction is $0.52$ on Algebra~I, $0.48$ on Algebra~II, $0.36$ on the geometry lower-central set, and $0.37$ on EQAO grade~6. Recomputed on the faithful subset (Table~\ref{tab:faithful}), Algebra~I masked-stem cleared on all three models at $n=184$, Algebra~II cleared chance but not the modal-letter bar at $n=115$, geometry lower-central kept its rate and lost its interval at $n=99$, and EQAO grade~6 cleared at $n=14$. In Algebra~I, 40 percent of faithful items have word options against 10 percent of not-faithful items, so the filter selects items as well as removing dirt.

The repaired layer removes that selection for the two algebra populations. Of 606 items, 431 are repaired-verified and 175 are not (Algebra~I 263 and 95; Algebra~II 168 and 80). Of the 431, 246 had been print-faithful and 185 had not: the repair recovers 185 items the filter dropped, and 53 items the filter kept do not verify. Fifteen items have no usable transcription and 17 more have no withheld quantity in the transcribed stem, so the withheld-quantity repaired population is 574 items, 343 Algebra~I and 231 Algebra~II, with 261 and 167 verified, verified fractions of $0.73$ and $0.68$ against the filter's $0.52$ and $0.48$. The first transcription also replaces the options of the two catalog rows where it is stored (277 of 279 geometry and 38 of 40 EQAO grade~6 fired items).

\subsection{Language models denied the given quantities}
\label{sec:partial-input}

\paragraph{Masked stem plus options.}
The withheld-quantity population on the text layer is 1{,}215 of 1{,}487 clean scored items, of which Algebra~I contributes 358 and Algebra~II 248; on the repaired layer 574 of the 606 algebra items keep a usable transcription and a withheld quantity, 261 and 167 of them verified. Table~\ref{tab:repaired} gives the rows on all three layers. On the repaired-verified population Algebra~I is $141/261=0.540$ $[0.479, 0.598]$ on Qwen 7B, $144/261=0.552$ $[0.494, 0.613]$ on Qwen 14B, and $129/261=0.494$ $[0.437, 0.559]$ on Phi-4, against chance $0.250$ and modal B $0.287$. Algebra~II is $75/167=0.449$ $[0.371, 0.527]$, $72/167=0.431$ $[0.353, 0.503]$, and $69/167=0.413$ $[0.335, 0.491]$, against chance $0.250$ and modal C $0.275$. All six rows clear both bars, and all six clear again on repaired-all (Algebra~I $n=343$, modal C $0.271$; Algebra~II $n=231$, modal C $0.286$). One-sided binomial $p$ against chance is below $10^{-16}$ for every Algebra~I row and below $10^{-5}$ for every Algebra~II row; the pre-registered bar is the bootstrap interval, and those $p$ values are a description.

The layers matter to the rows. The repaired Algebra~I rates ($0.540$, $0.552$, $0.494$) are above the text-layer rates ($0.388$, $0.416$, $0.422$) on every model: the dirty layer deflated this row. Algebra~II had cleared on the text layer ($0.367$, $0.359$, $0.347$ at $n=248$) and failed the modal-letter bar on its 115 print-faithful items, where the point estimates ($0.348$, $0.383$, $0.348$) stayed close to the text-layer rates but the lower bounds ($0.261$, $0.287$, $0.270$) fell below that subset's modal frequency $0.296$; the filter had removed items, not the effect, and the repaired layer, at 167 verified items, clears the bar on every model.

Holm at $\alpha=0.05$ in the 68-test language-model family, against chance on the text layer, rejects both Qwen Algebra~I rows, both Qwen Algebra~II rows, Phi-4 Algebra~I, Phi-4 Algebra~II, Phi-4 geometry, and Qwen 7B TEKS grade~8; the repaired layer is a rescoring of two of those cells on a corrected text and adds no test. Geometry on Phi-4 meets the text-layer cell bar ($67/166=0.404$) but not the split on items whose key is not the cell's modal letter ($0.229$, $n=118$, CI $[0.161, 0.305]$) and is not claimed; Qwen 7B clears Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) grade~5 and grade~8 on that scorer only; Qwen 14B TIMSS knowing rejects Holm against chance and does not beat its population modal $0.409$. None of those cells was re-read from the page, and none is claimed (Table~\ref{tab:masked}). Scorers are not averaged: the claim is that a reader exists in the Qwen2.5 family and one outside it, Phi-4, a 14-billion-parameter instruct model, and that a weaker reader does not. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, on the text layer, is $0.302$ $[0.257, 0.349]$ on Algebra~I, which clears chance but not modal B $0.271$, and $0.262$ $[0.210, 0.319]$ on Algebra~II, which clears neither; its with-stem letter-argmax ceiling matches its masked-stem rate (Algebra~I $0.304$, Algebra~II $0.290$; Table~\ref{tab:scorer}), so that miss is capability, not a missing cue. One human rater on the pre-registered 40-item Algebra~I masked-stem sheet scored $15/40=0.375$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.225, 0.525]$), an uninformative null; the claim is a model fact.

\paragraph{Option-numeral isomorphs.}
The masking leaves the option strings untouched and released Regents forms are widely mirrored, so a memorized item could carry the rate. Under the rank-preserving isomorph control (Section~\ref{sec:families}), with the original and the perturbed letters scored in the same run from the same model load so that no reproduction floor applies, every row clears the bar on the repaired-verified population (Table~\ref{tab:isomorph}): Algebra~I $0.500$ $[0.438, 0.562]$, $0.465$ $[0.404, 0.527]$, and $0.419$ $[0.354, 0.477]$ against modal B $0.288$; Algebra~II $0.412$ $[0.339, 0.485]$, $0.394$ $[0.321, 0.473]$, and $0.382$ $[0.315, 0.461]$ against modal C $0.273$. Four items whose perturbation raised or timed out are excluded ($n$ becomes 260 and 165 verified, 342 and 228 all); items whose options carry no numeral are unchanged and stay in the rate. The paired drop on items with an option numeral is $-0.049$ $[-0.117, 0.022]$ for Qwen 7B, $-0.103$ $[-0.166, -0.040]$ for Qwen 14B, and $-0.090$ $[-0.157, -0.013]$ for Phi-4 on Algebra~I ($n=223$ paired), and includes zero for all three on Algebra~II. Every scorer therefore has a form component that survives a change of every option value, on both cells, and Qwen 14B and Phi-4 also have a numeral-specific component of about $0.1$ on Algebra~I, which could be memorized option values or value cues that survive the masking, such as an option whose magnitude fits the question type; the control does not separate the two. Had the rank-preserving rate fallen to chance, the original rate would not have been a form effect; it did not on any scorer or cell. The text-layer control, on which Qwen 14B was excluded by a reproduction floor, is audit trail (Tables~\ref{tab:isomorph-text} and \ref{tab:amend14b}).

\paragraph{Contamination probes.}
On the text layer, 7B masked-stem accuracy by quartile of mean token log-probability of the original unmasked stem plus options is $0.349$ in Q1 versus $0.439$ in Q4 (Spearman $0.091$; 14B Spearman $0.107$, Q4 minus Q1 $0.120$). Recency on all 1{,}487 masked-stem 7B items is flat: $0.416$ / $0.409$ / $0.399$ / $0.402$ across before 2015, 2015 to 2019, 2020 to 2023, and 2024 and later ($n=178$ / $579$ / $383$ / $331$). Option permutation on the withheld-quantity items (7B) is $0.385$ $[0.357, 0.412]$ versus $0.376$ unpermuted; Algebra~I is $0.405$ $[0.355, 0.453]$. Version-2 masking on the version-1 withheld-quantity overlap is $0.370$ versus $0.376$, delta $-0.007$ (McNemar 50 version-2-only correct, 58 version-1-only), so extra masking does not create the channel. The probes and the isomorph control together do not support memorization of the items as the whole effect; they do not rule out a memorized share.

\paragraph{Which items carry the rows.}
Table~\ref{tab:verbclass} joins the two codings of Section~\ref{sec:codings-method} to the repaired-verified rows. By the verb class of the publisher's tag, the execution-tagged Algebra~I class ($n=130$) clears on all three models ($0.546$, $0.515$, $0.477$ against modal B $0.323$), as does the recognition-tagged class ($n=105$: $0.514$, $0.543$, $0.495$ against modal B $0.267$); on the filtered subset the execution-tagged class had cleared on no model at $n=75$, and the repaired layer reverses that. On Algebra~II the execution-tagged class ($n=83$) clears on all three and the recognition-tagged class ($n=57$) on Qwen only (Phi-4 $0.368$ $[0.246, 0.491]$).

The item-level coders agree with each other moderately (Cohen's $\kappa=0.576$ on 356 items, observed agreement $0.789$) and barely with the cluster split ($\kappa=0.071$ and $0.230$; observed agreement $0.461$ and $0.564$). Both see execution demand in more items than the tag does: 212 and 249 items coded execution against 164 execution-tagged, and of the 151 items tagged recognition or interpretation, 86 are coded execution by each coder and 79 by both, while 18 to 41 of the 164 execution-tagged items are coded recognition. The tag class is therefore not a population of execution items, and we lean on the intersections. On the 142 verified items both coders code execution, Qwen 7B ($0.401$ $[0.324, 0.486]$) and Qwen 14B ($0.437$ $[0.359, 0.521]$) clear the bar against modal B $0.303$; Phi-4 ($0.352$ $[0.275, 0.430]$) clears chance and not the modal-letter frequency, and on repaired-all ($n=188$) clears by $0.011$ (lower bound $0.298$ against modal C $0.287$), which is not a margin to claim on. On the 79 items that both coders and the tag mark as execution, only Qwen 7B clears. On the 61 items both coders code recognition or interpretation, all three models clear at $0.738$, $0.705$, and $0.705$ against modal A $0.295$, and those rates survive the option-value change ($0.639$, $0.689$, $0.607$); the recognition-coded items carry the highest rates on every model.

The statement we make is therefore the one about withheld information: on the items a claimed model matches, a pass does not require the given quantities. The stronger statement, that on items requiring operation on the givens a pass does not require executing that operation, would need the both-coders execution subset to clear on all three scorers on the verified population, and Phi-4 does not clear it; a further coding or a third scorer that cleared it on every scorer would replace the first statement with the second. The reader also uses cues outside the catalog. On the text layer, Qwen 14B Algebra~I scores $0.341$ when the key is the lower-central option ($n=82$) against $0.438$ when it is not ($n=276$), $0.454$ versus $0.402$ for the modal letter ($n=97$ / $261$), $0.370$ versus $0.423$ for the hub ($n=46$ / $312$), $0.333$ versus $0.427$ for the unique longest option ($n=42$ / $316$), and $0.442$ $[0.368, 0.521]$ on the 163 items with none of the four cues, against chance $0.250$; Phi-4 on the same residual is $0.380$ $[0.307, 0.460]$. By option type, Qwen 14B scores algebraic expressions $0.462$ ($n=65$), text $0.452$ ($n=188$), numeric $0.310$ ($n=71$), mixed $0.385$ ($n=26$), and ordered pairs $0.250$ ($n=8$); Phi-4 scores algebraic $0.369$, text $0.473$, and numeric $0.366$ $[0.254, 0.479]$, which clears chance.

\paragraph{An item with the quantities hidden.}
New York Regents Algebra~I, January 2023, item 22 (id \path{nyregents-algebra-i-2023-jan-q22}), which NYSED maps to cluster \texttt{A-APR.B}, ``understand the relationship between zeros and factors of polynomials'', a recognition-or-interpretation tag; both item-level coders code it recognition or interpretation. Masked stem, with the options as printed on the page:

{\small
\begin{quote}
If the zeros of the function $g(x)$ are $\{[N],[N],[N]\}$, which function could represent $g(x)$?

A.\ $g(x)=(x+3)(x-4)$\\
B.\ $g(x)=(x-3)(x+4)$\\
C.\ $g(x)=x(x+3)(x-4)$\\
D.\ $g(x)=x(x-3)(x+4)$
\end{quote}
}

Published key: C. On the repaired layer Qwen 7B and Qwen 14B pick C and Phi-4 picks D. The visible cue is the three placeholders in the zero set, which match the three-factor options that include an $x$: a reading of $n$ factors from $n$ placeholders. The reader may well apply the zeros-and-factors relation to the form of the options; what it cannot do is compute on the given zeros, which it never sees, and a correct answer here is read as evidence that the student matched the printed zeros to factors. In the census text layer of this form, $=$, $+$, and $-$ are non-printing control bytes and the stem's minus sign is lost; the page transcription restores the operators. The two transcriptions disagree (one drops the $g(x)=$ prefix from each option), so the item is in the repaired-all population and not in the verified one.

\subsection{Programs denied the tagged operation}
\label{sec:census}

\paragraph{Catalog rules.}
We ran 350{,}535 local trials. Fifteen a priori rules on every cell give the 105-test family (one PISA numeric unit-rate row with chance $0$ excluded as degenerate); five tests clear the per-cell bar on the text layer, and none survives Holm or Bonferroni at $\alpha=0.05$ (Bonferroni cut $0.00048$). There is no family-wise a priori discovery. Two of the five are lower-central, with one-sided binomial $p$ of $0.0038$ (New York Regents geometry, $90/279$) and $0.00070$ (EQAO grade~6, $20/40$) on the text layer. On options re-read from the page image both clear the per-cell bar (Table~\ref{tab:witnesses}): geometry $80/246=0.325$, item interval $[0.264, 0.382]$, and EQAO grade~6 $18/38=0.474$, item interval $[0.316, 0.632]$. On the filtered subset geometry had kept its rate and lost its interval at $n=99$ and EQAO grade~6 had cleared at $n=14$; the page-read options restore the $n$ the filter removed. The fired $n$ is the number of items on which at least three option numbers parse: 279 of 408 geometry items and 40 of 53 EQAO grade~6 items on the text layer, 246 and 38 on the page-read options (two items in each cell have no stored transcription, and the geometry rule stops firing on 31 items whose transcribed options carry fewer than three numbers). On the text layer, 19 of 27 geometry administrations have a rate above chance (sign test $p=0.014$), only one clears the item bar on its own, and the rate is the same on faithful ($0.323$, $n=99$) and not-faithful ($0.326$, $n=178$) items, so the dirt moved $n$ and not the rate; all three EQAO years are above chance by rate and one clears individually, and the $k=3$ clustered interval is descriptive. The geometry cue is central-ness: upper-central $0.330$ ($n=279$, $[0.272, 0.387]$) and nearest-to-mean $0.312$ also clear, smallest and largest ($0.219$ each) do not. On EQAO grade~6 only the pre-registered index clears; upper-central is $0.250$ $[0.125, 0.400]$, so that witness is specific to the second-smallest option. Both rows are per-cell existence rows on the page-read options that no Holm family keeps; they are replication candidates, and on the 80 and 18 matched items, matching the key does not require the tagged operation.

\subsection{TIMSS and the controls}
\label{sec:timss}

No catalog, strategy, or partial-input program clears its bar on TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning. Lower-central on TIMSS reasoning is $0.368$, $n=57$, $[0.246, 0.491]$ (Table~\ref{tab:middle}); elimination on reasoning is $0.379$ ($n=66$, $[0.258, 0.500]$ versus effective chance $0.263$); the portfolio rows are in Table~\ref{tab:portfolio}; the masked-stem rows are in Table~\ref{tab:masked}, where Qwen 14B knowing ($0.591$, $n=22$) rejects Holm against chance and does not beat the population modal $0.409$, and the first-run knowing rate $0.657$ ($n=35$) was carried by items where nothing was withheld. TIMSS 2011 is mostly removed from the language-model population by country-table contamination (17 items kept).

That null is power-limited. Table~\ref{tab:power} records, for every cell with $n\ge 10$, the smallest observed pass rate whose item-level bootstrap interval excludes chance at the cell's tagged-item count (the independent-items floor) and the bypass rate with 80\% power to clear the bar when administrations are resampled (the clustered floor). Twenty-one cells have $n\ge 10$; the smallest independent floor is $0.284$ on Algebra~I ($n=598$), whose clustered floor is $0.303$. The TIMSS cells pool the 2011 cycle with earlier released cycles at $n=81/68/86$ tagged and $65/54/68$ selected-response items; their independent floors are $0.358/0.353/0.372$ and their clustered floors $0.415/0.426/0.441$. A bypass rate in the low thirties on those cells would not have been seen.

The null is not a failure of the searcher. On 1{,}200 synthetic four-option items, each planted atomic program scored $50/50$ on holdout with confidence interval $[1, 1]$, recall of planted flaw types is $6/6$, and the false-positive rate on matched clean holdout is 0; that shows the searcher fires when a unique surface cue is planted, not that released items contain one. On public cue sets (Table~\ref{tab:controls}), the same 1{,}847-program space, fit on a train split and scored held-out, recovers documented shallow cues on OpenBookQA and SWAG, two corpora whose authors published an options-only or question-ignored number.
