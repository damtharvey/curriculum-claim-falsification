\subsection{Controls}

On 1{,}200 synthetic four-option items, each planted atomic program scored $50/50$ on holdout, confidence interval $[1, 1]$. Recall of planted flaw types is $6/6$. False-positive rate on matched clean holdout is 0. That shows the searcher fires when a unique surface cue is planted. It does not show that real released items contain such cues.

On public cue sets, the same 1{,}847-program space, fit on a train split and scored held-out, is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:controls}. The searcher recovers documented shallow cues on at least two corpora whose authors already published an options-only or question-ignored number. A TIMSS null is therefore not a failure of the tool to find anything.

\subsection{Power}

A claim cannot clear the bar below $n=10$. For larger $n$, Table~\ref{tab:power} records two floors. The independent-items floor is the smallest observed pass rate whose item-level bootstrap interval excludes chance, at the cell's tagged-item count. The clustered floor is the bypass rate with 80\% power to clear that bar when administrations are resampled. Twenty-one cells in this census have $n\ge 10$. The smallest independent-items floor is $0.284$, on New York Regents Algebra~I ($n=598$); that cell's clustered floor is $0.303$. Public-release TIMSS items tagged knowing, applying, or reasoning pool the 2011 cycle with earlier released cycles at $n=81/68/86$ tagged items and $n=65/54/68$ selected-response items. Independent-item floors on the tagged counts are $0.358/0.353/0.372$. Clustered 80\% floors on the selected-response counts, from administration resampling in \texttt{exports/addendum/power-by-cell.json}, are $0.415/0.426/0.441$. Those TIMSS cells are a null at $n$ of about 50 to 80.

\subsection{Census}

We ran 350{,}535 local trials. Fitted stem $n$-gram and overlap programs, state to TIMSS and the reverse, do not beat chance on holdout (state to TIMSS applying and reasoning: $36/122 = 0.295$, interval $[0.213, 0.377]$, chance $0.254$). Unbound unit-rate, $y=kx$, and percent-of do not clear the bar on TIMSS, PISA, or NAPLAN numeric items.

No searched or a priori program cleared the bar on TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning. Failure to find a witness on public selected-response $n$ of 65, 54, and 68 (clustered floors $0.415$, $0.426$, and $0.441$; independent-item floors $0.353$ to $0.372$) does not certify those tags. It shows that this program space, on these public items, did not refute them.

Fifteen catalog rules were scored on every authority-by-claim cell. The a priori family is every (rule, cell) pair with $n$ scored at least 10 and a positive format chance: 105 tests. One PISA numeric unit-rate row with chance $0$ was excluded as degenerate. Five of those 105 tests clear the pre-registered per-cell bar. None of the five survives Holm or Bonferroni at $\alpha=0.05$ (Bonferroni cut $0.00048$). There is no family-wise a priori discovery. Two of the five remain witnesses at the per-cell bar and are candidates for replication, not family-wise discoveries (Table~\ref{tab:witnesses}). Both are the lower-central signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Their one-sided binomial $p$ values are $0.0038$ (geometry, $90/279$) and $0.00070$ (EQAO grade 6, $20/40$).

The claimed $n$ is the number of items on which the rule fires (at least three parseable option numbers): 279 of 408 New York Regents geometry items and 40 of 53 EQAO grade~6 items. The remaining items in those cells are not scored by this rule.

\subsection{Adaptive strategy channels}
\label{sec:strategies}

The five strategy programs in Section~\ref{sec:method} are a second family: 67 tests, hashed before scoring, Holm inside the family. Three rows clear the per-cell bar. A 172-test pool with the 105 catalog tests is a sensitivity: the same single Holm reject, and none of the original 105 become Holm rejects. Catalog, strategy, and language-model tests are three Holm families, never one mixed pool.

\paragraph{Arithmetic closure (unbound execution).}
On STAAR grade~5 the depth-2 closure fires on 40 of 60 selected items and matches 21 of those 40 keys ($0.525$, $n=40$, item 95\% CI $[0.375, 0.675]$, chance $0.250$, one-sided binomial $p=0.000175$). That $p$ is the smallest in the 67-test family (Holm threshold $0.05/67=0.000746$) and remains the only Holm reject in that family and in the 172-test programmatic sensitivity (threshold $0.05/172=0.000291$). It is a discovery-set Holm reject on the frozen 2019/2021/2022 census. It did not replicate on held-out administrations, below. The catalog unit-rate row on the same cell ($0.417$, $n=12$, chance $0.146$) was already scored and unclaimed; closure is a different program with larger $n$, not a second independent mechanism. Grade~5 is the only closure cell that clears on the discovery set. Geometry is $0.196$ ($n=224$, interval $[0.143, 0.250]$), which does not clear.

The registered rule still holds under the pre-registered robustness cuts: depth~1 only is $17/30=0.567$ $[0.400, 0.733]$; depth~2 without the geometry extras $\pi$ and $ab/2$ is $20/40=0.500$ $[0.350, 0.650]$. Lower-central alone on the same 40 items is $13/40=0.325$ $[0.200, 0.475]$ and does not clear. Random tie-breaks among minimum-depth options give expected rate $0.367$ with a 1{,}000-draw interval $[0.250, 0.475]$; observed $0.525$ sits above that interval. Twenty-seven of the 40 picks were settled by the lower-central tie-break among minimum-depth options (15 of those 27 correct); 13 were settled by depth alone. Mean closure density is $3.3$ of 4 options. Clustered by STAAR year the interval is $[0.455, 0.615]$: 2019 $8/13$, 2021 $8/16$, 2022 $5/11$. The sign test on three administrations above chance is $p=0.125$. Those three forms are the whole cell.

Thirteen of the 40 fired stems contain artifact numerals (axis ticks, thousands separators, stem-and-leaf digits, footer grade numbers, leaked neighboring text). That is a construct problem for those 13. The remaining 27, a population defined after the fact, are $16/27=0.593$ $[0.370, 0.778]$. Unique depth-1 closure (exactly one option in the depth-1 set) is 10 of 40 fired, and that unique option is the key on 6 of those 10; $n=10$ is the bar edge. The frozen census row remains the Holm reject in the 67-test discovery family. It is not a confirmed family-wise discovery.

\subsection{Partial-input language-model channel}
\label{sec:partial-input}

A mechanical clean-option filter keeps 1{,}496 of 2{,}245 selected-response items, dropping exam footers, STAAR item codes, country-table fragments, options longer than 120 characters, and bleed from the next stem. TIMSS 2011 is mostly removed by country-table contamination (17 kept). Scorers take the letter with the highest next-token log-probability, with no chain of thought. The witness bar for this channel is $n\ge 10$ and a lower 95\% interval strictly above both format chance and the cell's modal-letter frequency, on items where at least one quantity was withheld. A pass on those items does not require processing the givens. It is not a claim that the model reasons, and not a claim that students guess. One rater on the pre-registered 40-item Algebra~I masked-stem sheet scored $15/40=0.375$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.225, 0.525]$, no items marked recognized). The lower bound is not strictly above chance $0.250$ or the cell modal frequency $0.271$; clearing at $n=40$ needs $k=17$. That is an uninformative null, not a human witness. Qwen 14B on the same 40 is $0.425$; on the 28 items whose census row matches the printed packet, Cohen's $\kappa$ is $0.212$ (human $9/28$, 14B $14/28$). The Algebra~I and Algebra~II language-model claim is a model fact and is unchanged.

\paragraph{Options only.}
On the 1{,}487 scoreable clean items, both local models score $0.301$ versus chance $0.250$ and always-B $0.275$ (7B interval $[0.277, 0.326]$; 14B $[0.276, 0.323]$). Letter agreement between models is $0.457$. A with-stem sanity check on 7B is $0.460$ on 200 items. A proprietary assistant scored through an audited no-tool transcript scores $0.359$ ($n=1493$, interval $[0.334, 0.384]$). No $n\ge 10$ cell has a 7B lower interval above that cell's modal-letter frequency. Algebra~I is $0.309$ $[0.265, 0.355]$ versus modal B $0.272$ (111 of 408). On the 1{,}026 items whose key is not the cell modal letter, 7B is $0.318$ $[0.288, 0.347]$ versus chance $0.250$. On 14B, Algebra~I is $0.321$ $[0.279, 0.363]$ versus modal $0.272$: the lower interval exceeds the modal frequency. That options-only Algebra~I row is not a claimed witness; the claimed object is the masked-stem condition below. Recency on the proprietary scorer minus 7B is largest before 2015 ($+0.112$, $n=178$), then $+0.024$ in 2015--2019 ($n=578$) and $+0.069$ in 2020 and later ($n=712$).

\subsection{What would refute these claims}

The two catalog lower-central claims would be false if the published keys on the scored items were wrong, if the implemented rule picked any index other than $\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor$ on the sorted parsed option values, or if a recomputed lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval on the same correct/incorrect flags did not exceed chance, including the administration-clustered interval. A family-wise Holm miss is not a refutation of the per-cell bar; it is reported above. An EQAO witness under upper-central or nearest-to-mean would also change the specification claim on that row.

The STAAR grade~5 closure discovery would be false if the published keys on those 40 fired items were wrong, if the implemented depth-2 closure with the registered tie-break did not produce 21 correct, or if the one-sided binomial $p$ against chance $0.25$ were larger than the Holm threshold on 67. That row is a discovery-set Holm reject that failed replication: the registered prediction on held-out filtered grade~5 items was a lower 95\% CI strictly above $0.250$, and the powered combined set is $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$. Artifact numerals on 13 discovery stems and the three-administration sign test remain caveats on the discovery construct.

The Algebra~I and Algebra~II masked-stem claims would be false if a recomputed lower 95\% interval on the withheld-quantity items did not exceed both chance and the cell modal-letter frequency on that scorer, or if the masking left a computable given quantity in the stem. A Holm miss against chance in the 68-test family would remove the family-wise statement; it would not by itself remove the per-cell channel bar if the interval still cleared. Mistral failing the same bar does not refute the Qwen or Phi-4 rows. A with-stem ceiling at the masked-stem rate, as on Mistral, is a capability miss, not a missing-cue miss.

A TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning witness would have been any catalog, strategy, or partial-input program that clears the bar stated for its channel, including the withheld-quantity language-model bar. No such row exists in this file. Public selected-response $n$ is 65, 54, and 68. Bypass rates below the clustered floors in Table~\ref{tab:power} would not have been seen at that $n$.
