\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every rate in this census was first computed on the PDF text layer of the released form. That layer often differs from the printed page (Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}), so the language-model rows and the two catalog rows that cleared on it were measured again on two further layers: a filter to the items whose text layer matches the page, and a repaired layer in which the stem and options are re-read from the page image. The repaired layer is the layer on which claims are made in this section; the filtered subset and the text layer are the audit trail (Appendix~\ref{app:trail}).

\subsection{Controls}

On 1{,}200 synthetic four-option items, each planted atomic program scored $50/50$ on holdout, confidence interval $[1, 1]$. Recall of planted flaw types is $6/6$. False-positive rate on matched clean holdout is 0. That shows the searcher fires when a unique surface cue is planted. It does not show that real released items contain such cues.

On public cue sets, the same 1{,}847-program space, fit on a train split and scored held-out, is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:controls}. The searcher recovers documented shallow cues on at least two corpora whose authors already published an options-only or question-ignored number. A TIMSS null is therefore not a failure of the tool to find anything.

\subsection{Power}

A claim cannot clear the bar below $n=10$. For larger $n$, Table~\ref{tab:power} records two floors. The independent-items floor is the smallest observed pass rate whose item-level bootstrap interval excludes chance, at the cell's tagged-item count. The clustered floor is the bypass rate with 80\% power to clear that bar when administrations are resampled. Twenty-one cells in this census have $n\ge 10$. The smallest independent-items floor is $0.284$, on New York Regents Algebra~I ($n=598$); that cell's clustered floor is $0.303$. Public-release TIMSS items tagged knowing, applying, or reasoning pool the 2011 cycle with earlier released cycles at $n=81/68/86$ tagged items and $n=65/54/68$ selected-response items. Independent-item floors on the tagged counts are $0.358/0.353/0.372$. Clustered 80\% floors on the selected-response counts, from administration resampling in \path{exports/addendum/power-by-cell.json}, are $0.415/0.426/0.441$. Those TIMSS cells are a null at $n$ of about 50 to 80.

\subsection{Partial-input language-model channel}
\label{sec:partial-input}

A mechanical clean-option filter keeps 1{,}496 of 2{,}245 selected-response items, dropping exam footers, STAAR item codes, country-table fragments, options longer than 120 characters, and bleed from the next stem. TIMSS 2011 is mostly removed by country-table contamination (17 kept). Scorers take the letter with the highest next-token log-probability, with no chain of thought. The witness bar for this channel is $n\ge 10$ and a lower 95\% interval strictly above both format chance and the cell's modal-letter frequency, on items where at least one quantity was withheld. A pass on those items does not require processing the givens. It is not a claim that the model reasons, and not a claim that students guess.

This is the information-restricted family: the policy has the operations and is denied the given quantities, where the catalog, strategy, and image-backsolving policies have the inputs and are denied the operation (Section~\ref{sec:method}). A bypass here refutes the claim that the pass requires computing on these givens. It does not refute the claim that the pass requires the relation the tag names; the model may apply that relation to the form of the options, and the item codings below are how we ask which items it does that on. Score use rests on the first claim: the key is read as evidence that the student computed on the givens.

The claimed rows in this channel are on the repaired layer of Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}. For every New York Regents Algebra~I and Algebra~II item in the withheld-quantity population, the stem and options were re-read from the page image by a vision-language model, and a second, independent transcription of the same page was compared to the first under the print-fidelity thresholds. The 431 of 606 items on which the two transcriptions agree are the \emph{repaired-verified} population; claims are made there. The full repaired population (\emph{repaired-all}) is a sensitivity. The text-layer rows and the print-faithful filtered rows are kept in Appendix~\ref{app:trail} as the audit trail. Other cells in this channel (geometry, EQAO, TIMSS, TEKS) were not re-read from the page, were measured on the text layer only, and none is claimed (Table~\ref{tab:masked}).

\paragraph{Masked stem plus options.}
Every numeral, number word, fraction, percentage, decimal, and value binding in the stem is replaced by $[N]$. Question wording, units, and options remain. The primary population is the 1{,}215 of 1{,}487 clean scored items on which the masker withheld at least one quantity; Algebra~I contributes 358 and Algebra~II 248. On the repaired layer, 574 of those 606 items keep a usable transcription and at least one withheld quantity (Algebra~I 343, Algebra~II 231), and 261 and 167 of them are verified. Table~\ref{tab:repaired} gives the rows. On the repaired-verified population Algebra~I is $141/261=0.540$ $[0.479, 0.598]$ on Qwen 7B, $144/261=0.552$ $[0.494, 0.613]$ on Qwen 14B, and $129/261=0.494$ $[0.437, 0.559]$ on Phi-4, against chance $0.250$ and modal B $0.287$. Algebra~II is $75/167=0.449$ $[0.371, 0.527]$ on Qwen 7B, $72/167=0.431$ $[0.353, 0.503]$ on Qwen 14B, and $69/167=0.413$ $[0.335, 0.491]$ on Phi-4, against chance $0.250$ and modal C $0.275$. All six rows clear both bars, and all six clear again on repaired-all (Algebra~I $n=343$, modal C $0.271$; Algebra~II $n=231$, modal C $0.286$). The claimed set is therefore Algebra~I and Algebra~II on Qwen 7B, Qwen 14B, and Phi-4.

Algebra~II fails on the print-faithful filter and clears on the repaired layer. On the text layer it cleared on all three models ($0.367$, $0.359$, $0.347$ at $n=248$). The print-faithful filter kept 115 of its 248 items, a subset selected toward administrations before 2023 and word options; there the point estimates ($0.348$, $0.383$, $0.348$) were close to the text-layer rates, but at half the $n$ the lower bounds ($0.261$, $0.287$, $0.270$) were above chance and below that subset's modal-letter frequency $0.296$. The filter had removed items, not the effect. The repaired layer scores 231 Algebra~II items, 167 of them verified, and the interval clears the position baseline on every model. The repaired Algebra~I rates ($0.540$, $0.552$, $0.494$ verified) are above the text-layer rates ($0.388$, $0.416$, $0.422$) on every model: the dirty layer deflated this row. On the repaired-verified population, one-sided binomial $p$ against chance is below $10^{-16}$ for every Algebra~I row and below $10^{-5}$ for every Algebra~II row; the pre-registered bar is the bootstrap interval, and those $p$ values are a description.

The language-model Holm family is every scored (model, cell) on the text-layer withheld-quantity masked-stem population with $n\ge 10$: 68 tests (four models, 17 cells). Options-only is a ladder rung, not a member of that family. Holm at $\alpha=0.05$ in that family, against chance, rejects both Qwen Algebra~I rows, both Qwen Algebra~II rows, Phi-4 Algebra~I, Phi-4 Algebra~II, Phi-4 geometry, and Qwen 7B TEKS grade~8. The repaired layer adds no test to that family: it is a rescoring of the same two cells on a corrected text. Scorers are not averaged. Geometry on Phi-4 meets the text-layer cell bar ($67/166=0.404$) but not the split on items whose key is not the cell's modal letter ($0.229$, $n=118$, CI $[0.161, 0.305]$) and is not claimed; Qwen 7B clears TEKS grade~5 and grade~8 on that scorer only; Qwen 14B TIMSS knowing rejects Holm against chance and does not beat its population modal $0.409$. Those text-layer rows and the options-only and information-ladder rungs are in Appendix~\ref{app:trail}. One rater on the pre-registered 40-item Algebra~I masked-stem sheet scored $15/40=0.375$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.225, 0.525]$); that is an uninformative null, not a human witness, and the claim is a model fact.

\paragraph{Which items carry the rows.}
Two codings of the items' demand are joined to the repaired-verified rows (Table~\ref{tab:verbclass}). The first is the pre-registered verb class of the publisher's tag (Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-method}): the CCSS cluster heading that NYSED assigns to the item names execution on given quantities, recognition or interpretation of a form, or both. On the repaired-verified Algebra~I population the execution-tagged class ($n=130$) clears on all three models ($0.546$, $0.515$, $0.477$ against modal B $0.323$), as does the recognition-tagged class ($n=105$: $0.514$, $0.543$, $0.495$ against modal B $0.267$). On the filtered subset the execution-tagged class had cleared on no model at $n=75$; the repaired layer, with 130 execution-tagged verified items, reverses that. On Algebra~II the execution-tagged class ($n=83$) clears on all three and the recognition-tagged class ($n=57$) clears on Qwen only (Phi-4 $0.368$ $[0.246, 0.491]$).

The second coding is item-level. Two language-model coders (GLM 5.2 and Kimi K3) each coded all 358 Algebra~I items for what answering the item requires, under one fixed rubric with three codes, execution (the key requires operating on the specific given quantities), recognition or interpretation (the key can be picked from the form, meaning, or property named in the wording), or mixed, blind to the key, to every model prediction, and to the cluster split (Appendix~\ref{app:coders}). The coders agree with each other moderately (Cohen's $\kappa=0.576$ on 356 items, observed agreement $0.789$) and barely with the cluster split ($\kappa=0.071$ and $0.230$; observed agreement $0.461$ and $0.564$). Both coders see execution demand in more items than the tag does: 212 and 249 items coded execution against 164 execution-tagged, and of the 151 items tagged recognition or interpretation, 86 are coded execution by each coder and 79 by both. The cluster tag is therefore a noisy proxy for what the item asks, and we lean on the intersections. On the 142 verified items both coders code execution, Qwen 7B ($0.401$ $[0.324, 0.486]$) and Qwen 14B ($0.437$ $[0.359, 0.521]$) clear the bar against modal B $0.303$; Phi-4 ($0.352$ $[0.275, 0.430]$) clears chance and not the modal-letter frequency. On the 79 items that both coders and the tag mark as execution, only Qwen 7B clears. On the 61 items both coders code recognition or interpretation, all three models clear at $0.738$, $0.705$, and $0.705$ against modal A $0.295$, and those rates survive the option-value change below ($0.639$, $0.689$, $0.607$).

So the statement we make is the one about withheld information: on the matched items, a pass does not require the given quantities. The stronger statement, that on items requiring operation on the givens a pass does not require executing the tagged operation on them, would need the both-coders execution subset to clear on all three scorers on the verified population, and Phi-4 does not clear it; on repaired-all ($n=188$) Phi-4 clears by $0.011$ (lower bound $0.298$ against modal C $0.287$), which is not a margin to claim on. The execution-tagged cluster class does clear on all three, but both item-level coders read execution demand into 79 of the 151 recognition-tagged items and recognition into 18 to 41 of the 164 execution-tagged ones, so the tag class is not a population of execution items. The recognition-coded items carry the highest rates on every model.

\paragraph{Option-numeral isomorphs.}
The masking leaves the option strings untouched, and released Regents forms are widely mirrored, so a memorized item could carry the rate. The pre-registered control (Appendix~\ref{app:lm}) rescores the same masked stems with every numeral in the option strings replaced by a value-changing, form-preserving isomorph: same digit count, sign, decimal places, and comma grouping, the same replacement wherever a literal repeats within the item, the rank order of the options' leading numbers preserved, and the key letter unchanged. On the repaired layer the original and the perturbed letters were scored in the same run from the same model load, so no reproduction floor applies, and all three scorers are covered. Four items whose perturbation raised or timed out are excluded ($n$ becomes 260 and 165 verified, 342 and 228 all); items whose options carry no numeral are unchanged and stay in the rate. Table~\ref{tab:isomorph} gives the rows. On the repaired-verified population every row clears the bar under the value change: Algebra~I $0.500$ $[0.438, 0.562]$, $0.465$ $[0.404, 0.527]$, and $0.419$ $[0.354, 0.477]$ against modal B $0.288$; Algebra~II $0.412$ $[0.339, 0.485]$, $0.394$ $[0.321, 0.473]$, and $0.382$ $[0.315, 0.461]$ against modal C $0.273$. The paired drop on items with an option numeral is $-0.049$ $[-0.117, 0.022]$ for Qwen 7B, $-0.103$ $[-0.166, -0.040]$ for Qwen 14B, and $-0.090$ $[-0.157, -0.013]$ for Phi-4 on Algebra~I ($n=223$ paired), and includes zero for all three on Algebra~II. So every scorer has a form component that survives a change of every option value, on both cells, and Qwen 14B and Phi-4 also have a numeral-specific component of about $0.1$ on Algebra~I. That component could be memorized option values or value cues that survive the masking, such as an option whose magnitude fits the question type; the control does not separate the two. The text-layer control, on which Qwen 14B was not scored because its re-scored letters fell below a reproduction floor, is kept as audit trail in Appendix~\ref{app:trail} (Tables~\ref{tab:isomorph-text} and \ref{tab:amend14b}).

\paragraph{Contamination probes.}
The remaining probes are on the text layer. On the withheld-quantity items, 7B masked-stem accuracy by quartile of mean token log-probability of the original unmasked stem plus options is $0.349$ in Q1 versus $0.439$ in Q4 (Spearman $0.091$; 14B Spearman $0.107$, Q4 minus Q1 $0.120$). Recency on all 1{,}487 masked-stem 7B items is flat: $0.416$ / $0.409$ / $0.399$ / $0.402$ across before 2015, 2015 to 2019, 2020 to 2023, and 2024 and later ($n=178$ / $579$ / $383$ / $331$). Option permutation on the withheld-quantity items (7B) is $0.385$ $[0.357, 0.412]$ versus $0.376$ unpermuted; Algebra~I is $0.405$ $[0.355, 0.453]$. Version-2 masking (named figures, bindings, leftover coefficients) on the version-1 withheld-quantity overlap is $0.370$ versus $0.376$, delta $-0.007$ (McNemar 50 version-2-only correct, 58 version-1-only). Extra masking does not create the channel. These probes and the isomorph control together do not support memorization of the items as the whole effect; they do not rule out a memorized share.

\paragraph{Scorer dependence.}
The claim is existential. A reader exists in the Qwen2.5 family (7B-Instruct and 14B-Instruct) and one outside it (Phi-4, a 14-billion-parameter instruct model) that recovers the key from question type plus option form on withheld-quantity Algebra~I and Algebra~II items re-read from the page. A weaker reader does not. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (revision in Appendix~\ref{app:lm}), scored on the text layer only, is $0.302$ $[0.257, 0.349]$ on Algebra~I, which clears chance $0.250$ but not modal B $0.271$, and $0.262$ $[0.210, 0.319]$ on Algebra~II, which clears neither; its with-stem letter-argmax ceiling matches its masked-stem rate (Algebra~I $0.304$, Algebra~II $0.290$; Table~\ref{tab:scorer}), so that miss is capability, not a missing cue. We do not average across scorers.

\paragraph{An item with the quantities hidden.}
New York Regents Algebra~I, January 2023, item 22, which NYSED maps to cluster \texttt{A-APR.B}, ``understand the relationship between zeros and factors of polynomials'' (a recognition-or-interpretation tag under the rule in Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-method}); both item-level coders code it recognition or interpretation.

The item id is \path{nyregents-algebra-i-2023-jan-q22}.

A.\ $g(x)=(x+3)(x-4)$\\
B.\ $g(x)=(x-3)(x+4)$\\
C.\ $g(x)=x(x+3)(x-4)$\\

}

Published key: C. On the repaired layer Qwen 7B and Qwen 14B pick C and Phi-4 picks D. The visible cue is the three placeholders in the zero set, which match the three-factor option that includes an $x$. That is a reading of $n$ factors from $n$ placeholders. The tag names the relationship between zeros and factors, and the reader may well apply that relationship to the form of the options; what it does not do is compute on the given zeros, because it never sees them. The bypass is therefore a witness that this pass does not require the given zeros, and not a witness that it omits the relation the tag names. The first is the claim score use rests on: a correct answer here is read as evidence that the student matched the printed zeros to factors, and the key can be matched without the zeros. The options are printed here as they appear on the page. In the census text layer of this January 2023 form, $=$, $+$, and $-$ are non-printing control bytes and the stem's minus sign is lost; the page transcription restores the operators. The two transcriptions of this item disagree (one drops the $g(x)=$ prefix from each option), so it is in the repaired-all population and not in the verified one.

\subsection{Adaptive strategy channels}
\label{sec:strategies}

The five strategy programs in Section~\ref{sec:method} are a second family: 67 tests, hashed before scoring, Holm inside the family. Three rows clear the per-cell bar. A 172-test pool with the 105 catalog tests is a sensitivity: the same single Holm reject, and none of the original 105 become Holm rejects. Catalog, strategy, and language-model tests are three Holm families, never one mixed pool.

\paragraph{Arithmetic closure (unbound execution).}
On STAAR grade~5 the depth-2 closure fires on 40 of 60 selected items and matches 21 of those 40 keys.
The rate is $0.525$ ($n=40$, item 95\% CI $[0.375, 0.675]$, chance $0.250$, one-sided binomial $p=0.000175$). That $p$ is the smallest in the 67-test family (Holm threshold $0.05/67=0.000746$) and remains the only Holm reject in that family and in the 172-test programmatic sensitivity (threshold $0.05/172=0.000291$). It is a discovery-set Holm reject on the frozen 2019/2021/2022 census. It did not replicate on held-out administrations, below. The catalog unit-rate row on the same cell ($0.417$, $n=12$, chance $0.146$) was already scored and unclaimed; closure is a different program with larger $n$, not a second independent mechanism. Grade~5 is the only closure cell that clears on the discovery set. Geometry is $0.196$ ($n=224$, interval $[0.143, 0.250]$), which does not clear.

\subsection{The text layer against the printed page}
\label{sec:print-audit}

A 20-item printed-page audit, 10 geometry lower-central hits and 10 Algebra~I masked-stem hits, compared each census row to the source exam PDF by hand (no optical character recognition). Nine rows match stem, options, and key. Eight have footer, figure-label, or previous-item glue on an option or stem. Two are truncated. One Algebra~I row has the printed stem and a different option set (June 2023 item 3: printed lines $y=2x-2$ and three neighbors; census inequalities). An earlier count of 8 matches and 3 truncations included one row (January 2023 item 8) whose stem the audit tool had printed as a 400-character preview; the census stem is complete and matches the print. No sampled PDF was missing. Glue on geometry option D is usually a footer after a correct leading number.

\paragraph{Filter.}
The mechanical check of Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-method} then classified all 955 items in the claimed cells: 434 print-faithful, 502 not faithful, 19 unverified. The text layer is wrong in two main ways: glue from footers, diagram labels, table cells, or the next item (241 items), and option sets or stems that do not match the printed item (230), most of them Regents forms from 2023 onward whose PDFs encode $=$, $+$, and $-$ as the digits 5, 1, and 2. The faithful fraction per claimed cell is $0.52$ (Algebra~I), $0.48$ (Algebra~II), $0.36$ (geometry lower-central), and $0.37$ (EQAO grade~6). Table~\ref{tab:faithful} in Appendix~\ref{app:trail} recomputes every row claimed on the text layer on the faithful subset and on its complement. Algebra~I masked-stem cleared on the 184 faithful items for all three models; Algebra~II did not clear the modal-letter bar on its 115; geometry lower-central kept its rate and lost its interval on 99; EQAO grade~6 cleared on 14. The faithful subset is not a random sample of any cell: in Algebra~I, 40 percent of faithful items have word options against 10 percent of not-faithful items, and administrations before 2023 dominate. It answers whether the effect exists on items whose text is what students saw; it does not estimate the rate on the cell, and a transcription mistake moves an item out of it, never in.

\paragraph{Repair.}
Filtering keeps a selected half of each cell. The repaired layer replaces the text layer instead. For all 606 Algebra~I and Algebra~II withheld-quantity items, the stem and options become the vision-language transcription of the page (transcription 1, the output already produced for the check above), and a second transcription of the same page is produced by the same model with a different prompt and a 6~pt larger crop (transcription 2). An item is \emph{repaired-verified} when the two transcriptions agree under the print-fidelity thresholds (stem token F1, precision, and recall at least $0.8$; every option at least $0.9$; equal option counts); the key letter is always the census key, never a model guess. The thresholds, prompt, and procedure were hashed before either the verification transcription or any scoring (Appendix~\ref{app:repair}). Of 606 items, 431 are verified and 175 are not (Algebra~I 263 and 95; Algebra~II 168 and 80). Of the 431, 246 had been print-faithful and 185 had been not faithful under the filter: the repair recovers 185 items the filter had dropped, and 53 items the filter had kept do not verify. Fifteen items have no usable transcription 1 and 17 more have no withheld quantity in the transcribed stem; the withheld-quantity repaired population is 574 items, 343 Algebra~I and 231 Algebra~II, with 261 and 167 verified. The verified population is not selected on whether the text layer matched the page; its remaining selection is what two readings of the page agree on, and its verified fraction by cell is $0.73$ and $0.68$ rather than the filter's $0.52$ and $0.48$. Every masked-stem, item-class, and isomorph row in Section~\ref{sec:partial-input} was then scored on that layer with the same models, seed, and letter rule. The two catalog rows use transcription 1 alone where it is stored (277 of 279 geometry and 38 of 40 EQAO grade~6 fired items): those options were not verified by a second transcription, and the rule re-fires on 246 and 38 of them (Table~\ref{tab:witnesses}).

\subsection{What would refute these claims}

The EQAO grade~6 and geometry lower-central rows would be false if the published keys on the scored items were wrong, if the implemented rule picked any index other than $\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor$ on the sorted parsed option values, or if a recomputed lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval on the same correct/incorrect flags on the 38 and 246 page-read items did not exceed chance. Both rows are per-cell existence rows that no Holm family keeps; a family-wise miss is reported above and is not a refutation of the per-cell bar. The EQAO row is specific to the second-smallest option; an EQAO witness under upper-central or nearest-to-mean would change the specification claim on that row. The page-read options for these two cells come from one transcription without a second reading; a second transcription that disagreed on enough items to move the interval onto chance would remove the row.

The STAAR grade~5 closure discovery would be false if the published keys on those 40 fired items were wrong, if the implemented depth-2 closure with the registered tie-break did not produce 21 correct, or if the one-sided binomial $p$ against chance $0.25$ were larger than the Holm threshold on 67. That row is a discovery-set Holm reject that failed replication: the registered prediction on held-out filtered grade~5 items was a lower 95\% CI strictly above $0.250$, and the powered combined set is $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$. Artifact numerals on 13 discovery stems and the three-administration sign test remain caveats on the discovery construct.

The Algebra~I and Algebra~II masked-stem claims would be false if a recomputed lower 95\% interval on the repaired-verified withheld-quantity items did not exceed both chance and that population's modal-letter frequency on that scorer, if the masking left a computable given quantity in the transcribed stem, or if the two page transcriptions that define the verified population were shown to share an error that put the key into the stem. A Holm miss against chance in the 68-test text-layer family would remove the family-wise statement; it would not by itself remove the per-cell channel bar if the interval still cleared. Mistral failing the same bar does not refute the Qwen or Phi-4 rows. A with-stem ceiling at the masked-stem rate, as on Mistral, is a capability miss, not a missing-cue miss. The option-numeral control tests the mechanism behind the claim rather than the claim: had the rank-preserving rate on the repaired-verified items fallen to chance, the original rate would not have been a form effect. It did not on any scorer or cell (Table~\ref{tab:isomorph}). The claim does not extend to a statement about the tagged operation: the both-coders execution subset clears on Qwen 7B and Qwen 14B and not on Phi-4, and if a further coding or a third scorer cleared it on every scorer, the information-withholding statement in Section~\ref{sec:partial-input} would be replaced by the stronger one.

The Algebra~I image-backsolve existence row would be false if honest $n$ fired were below 10, if the lower 95\% interval on those 15 flags were not strictly above chance $0.25$, or if the 14 hits were invented formulas rather than displayed equations. A Holm statement about this row is not claimed.

A TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning witness would have been any catalog, strategy, or partial-input program that clears the bar stated for its channel, including the withheld-quantity language-model bar. No such row exists in this file. Public selected-response $n$ is 65, 54, and 68. Bypass rates below the clustered floors in Table~\ref{tab:power} would not have been seen at that $n$.
