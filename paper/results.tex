\subsection{Controls}

On 1{,}200 synthetic four-option items, each planted atomic program scored $50/50$ on holdout, confidence interval $[1, 1]$. Recall of planted flaw types is $6/6$. False-positive rate on matched clean holdout is 0. That shows the searcher fires when a unique surface cue is planted. It does not show that real released items contain such cues.

On public cue sets, the same 1{,}847-program space, fit on a train split and scored held-out, is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:controls}. The searcher recovers documented shallow cues on at least two corpora whose authors already published an options-only or question-ignored number. A TIMSS null is therefore not a failure of the tool to find anything.

\subsection{Power}

A claim cannot clear the bar below $n=10$. For larger $n$, Table~\ref{tab:power} records two floors. The independent-items floor is the smallest observed pass rate whose item-level bootstrap interval excludes chance, at the cell's tagged-item count. The clustered floor is the bypass rate with 80\% power to clear that bar when administrations are resampled. Twenty-one cells in this census have $n\ge 10$. The smallest independent-items floor is $0.284$, on New York Regents Algebra~I ($n=598$); that cell's clustered floor is $0.303$. Public-release TIMSS items tagged knowing, applying, or reasoning pool the 2011 cycle with earlier released cycles at $n=81/68/86$ tagged items and $n=65/54/68$ selected-response items. Clustered 80\% floors on the selected-response counts, from administration resampling in \path{exports/addendum/power-by-cell.json}, are $0.415/0.426/0.441$. Those TIMSS cells are a null at $n$ of about 50 to 80.

\subsection{Census}

We ran 350{,}535 local trials. Fitted stem $n$-gram and overlap programs, state to TIMSS and the reverse, do not beat chance on holdout (state to TIMSS applying and reasoning: $36/122 = 0.295$, interval $[0.213, 0.377]$, chance $0.254$). Unbound unit-rate, $y=kx$, and percent-of do not clear the bar on TIMSS, PISA, or NAPLAN numeric items.

No searched or a priori program cleared the bar on TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning. Failure to find a witness on public selected-response $n$ of 65, 54, and 68 (clustered floors $0.415$, $0.426$, and $0.441$; independent-item floors $0.353$ to $0.372$) does not certify those tags. It shows that this program space, on these public items, did not refute them.

Fifteen catalog rules were scored on every authority-by-claim cell. The a priori family is every (rule, cell) pair with $n$ scored at least 10 and a positive format chance: 105 tests. One PISA numeric unit-rate row with chance $0$ was excluded as degenerate. Five of those 105 tests clear the pre-registered per-cell bar on the text layer. None of the five survives Holm or Bonferroni at $\alpha=0.05$ (Bonferroni cut $0.00048$). There is no family-wise a priori discovery. Two of the five clear the per-cell bar on the text layer (Table~\ref{tab:witnesses}); both are the lower-central signed variant of the catalog numeric-central-value cue in Section~\ref{sec:method}, with one-sided binomial $p$ values $0.0038$ (geometry, $90/279$) and $0.00070$ (EQAO grade 6, $20/40$). On print-faithful items (Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}) only one of the two still clears. EQAO grade~6 is $8/14=0.571$, item interval $[0.286, 0.857]$, and remains a candidate for replication at that thin $n$. Geometry keeps its rate, $32/99=0.323$, but the interval $[0.232, 0.414]$ covers chance; it is a text-layer residue, not a per-cell witness.

The fired $n$ is the number of items on which the rule fires (at least three parseable option numbers): 279 of 408 New York Regents geometry items and 40 of 53 EQAO grade~6 items. The remaining items in those cells are not scored by this rule.

\subsection{Adaptive strategy channels}
\label{sec:strategies}

The five strategy programs in Section~\ref{sec:method} are a second family: 67 tests, hashed before scoring, Holm inside the family. Three rows clear the per-cell bar. A 172-test pool with the 105 catalog tests is a sensitivity: the same single Holm reject, and none of the original 105 become Holm rejects. Catalog, strategy, and language-model tests are three Holm families, never one mixed pool.

\paragraph{Arithmetic closure (unbound execution).}
On STAAR grade~5 the depth-2 closure fires on 40 of 60 selected items and matches 21 of those 40 keys.
The rate is $0.525$ ($n=40$, item 95\% CI $[0.375, 0.675]$, chance $0.250$, one-sided binomial $p=0.000175$). That $p$ is the smallest in the 67-test family (Holm threshold $0.05/67=0.000746$) and remains the only Holm reject in that family and in the 172-test programmatic sensitivity (threshold $0.05/172=0.000291$). It is a discovery-set Holm reject on the frozen 2019/2021/2022 census. It did not replicate on held-out administrations, below. The catalog unit-rate row on the same cell ($0.417$, $n=12$, chance $0.146$) was already scored and unclaimed; closure is a different program with larger $n$, not a second independent mechanism. Grade~5 is the only closure cell that clears on the discovery set. Geometry is $0.196$ ($n=224$, interval $[0.143, 0.250]$), which does not clear.

The registered rule still holds under the pre-registered robustness cuts: depth~1 only is $17/30=0.567$ $[0.400, 0.733]$; depth~2 without the geometry extras $\pi$ and $ab/2$ is $20/40=0.500$ $[0.350, 0.650]$. Lower-central alone on the same 40 items is $13/40=0.325$ $[0.200, 0.475]$ and does not clear. Random tie-breaks among minimum-depth options give expected rate $0.367$ with a 1{,}000-draw interval $[0.250, 0.475]$; observed $0.525$ sits above that interval. Twenty-seven of the 40 picks were settled by the lower-central tie-break among minimum-depth options (15 of those 27 correct); 13 were settled by depth alone. Mean closure density is $3.3$ of 4 options. Clustered by STAAR year the interval is $[0.455, 0.615]$: 2019 $8/13$, 2021 $8/16$, 2022 $5/11$. The sign test on three administrations above chance is $p=0.125$. Those three forms are the whole cell.

Thirteen of the 40 fired stems contain artifact numerals (axis ticks, thousands separators, stem-and-leaf digits, footer grade numbers, leaked neighboring text). That is a construct problem for those 13. The remaining 27, a population defined after the fact, are $16/27=0.593$ $[0.370, 0.778]$. Unique depth-1 closure (exactly one option in the depth-1 set) is 10 of 40 fired, and that unique option is the key on 6 of those 10; $n=10$ is the bar edge. The frozen census row remains the Holm reject in the 67-test discovery family. It is not a confirmed family-wise discovery.

\paragraph{Replication on held-out STAAR grade~5.}
The registered prediction was that filtered arithmetic closure on new grade~5 items alone would have a lower 95\% CI strictly above $0.250$. On the version-1 unrepaired parse, 18 of 44 keys match ($0.409$, CI $[0.273, 0.545]$): the prediction is met, and $n=44$ is below the registered power floor of 46. That positive is not described as underpowered. After a mechanical last-option page-leak repair, hashed before rescoring (42 last-option repairs, 3 stacked-fraction rejoins; hand-check 14 agree / 4 disagree / 2 uncertain, 0 key mismatches), 17 of 44 match ($0.386$, CI $[0.250, 0.545]$). The prediction is not met. That null is underpowered ($n=44<46$). English grade~5 forms for 2017 and 2018 were then parsed (the 2015 key, the 2020 test, and the 2023 test were not found). New forms alone are 4 of 20 ($0.200$, CI $[0.050, 0.400]$). Combined repaired plus new is 21 of 64 ($0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$). Here $n=64\ge 46$. The prediction is not met, and this null is not underpowered. Grade~4 lower-central is still 10 of 17 after repair; 5 of those 10 hits are parse artifacts, and the clean 5 of 9 does not clear. It is not a witness. The Holm-surviving unbound-execution row on the frozen STAAR 2019/2021/2022 grade~5 cell did not replicate on held-out 2013/2014/2016/2017/2018 administrations after the pre-registered option-leak repair. Discovery $0.525$, combined replication $0.328$ with an interval that covers chance.

\subsection{Partial-input language-model channel}
\label{sec:partial-input}

A mechanical clean-option filter keeps 1{,}496 of 2{,}245 selected-response items, dropping exam footers, STAAR item codes, country-table fragments, options longer than 120 characters, and bleed from the next stem. TIMSS 2011 is mostly removed by country-table contamination (17 kept). Scorers take the letter with the highest next-token log-probability, with no chain of thought. The witness bar for this channel is $n\ge 10$ and a lower 95\% interval strictly above both format chance and the cell's modal-letter frequency, on items where at least one quantity was withheld. A pass on those items does not require processing the givens. It is not a claim that the model reasons, and not a claim that students guess. One rater on the pre-registered 40-item Algebra~I masked-stem sheet scored $15/40=0.375$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.225, 0.525]$, no items marked recognized). The lower bound is not strictly above chance $0.250$ or the cell modal frequency $0.271$; clearing at $n=40$ needs $k=17$. That is an uninformative null, not a human witness. Qwen 14B on the same 40 is $0.425$; on the 28 items whose census row matches the printed packet, Cohen's $\kappa$ is $0.212$ (human $9/28$, 14B $14/28$). The Algebra~I language-model claim is a model fact and is unchanged by the human sheet. Rates in this section are on the text layer unless a paragraph says print-faithful; the print-faithful recomputation is in Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}, and it removes Algebra~II.

\paragraph{Options only.}
On the 1{,}487 scoreable clean items, both local models score $0.301$ versus chance $0.250$ and always-B $0.275$ (7B interval $[0.277, 0.326]$; 14B $[0.276, 0.323]$). Letter agreement between models is $0.457$. A with-stem sanity check on 7B is $0.460$ on 200 items. A proprietary assistant scored through an audited no-tool transcript scores $0.359$ ($n=1493$, interval $[0.334, 0.384]$). No $n\ge 10$ cell has a 7B lower interval above that cell's modal-letter frequency. Algebra~I is $0.309$ $[0.265, 0.355]$ versus modal B $0.272$ (111 of 408). On the 1{,}026 items whose key is not the cell modal letter, 7B is $0.318$ $[0.288, 0.347]$ versus chance $0.250$. On 14B, Algebra~I is $0.321$ $[0.279, 0.363]$ versus modal $0.272$: the lower interval exceeds the modal frequency. That options-only Algebra~I row is not a claimed witness; the claimed object is the masked-stem condition below. Recency on the proprietary scorer minus 7B is largest before 2015 ($+0.112$, $n=178$), then $+0.024$ in 2015--2019 ($n=578$) and $+0.069$ in 2020 and later ($n=712$).

\paragraph{Masked stem plus options.}
Every numeral, number word, fraction, percentage, decimal, and value binding in the stem is replaced by $[N]$. Question wording, units, and options remain. Of 1{,}487 scored items, 272 had nothing withheld; the primary population is the other 1{,}215. First-run pooled rates on all 1{,}487 (7B $0.405$, 14B $0.425$) mix those 272 in and are not the claim. On the withheld-quantity population, Regents Algebra~I is $139/358=0.388$ $[0.341, 0.439]$ on 7B and $149/358=0.416$ $[0.363, 0.469]$ on 14B, versus chance $0.250$ and modal B $0.271$ (97 of 358). Algebra~II is $91/248=0.367$ $[0.306, 0.423]$ on 7B and $89/248=0.359$ $[0.298, 0.415]$ on 14B, versus chance $0.250$ and modal C $0.278$ (69 of 248). Both cells clear both bars on both models on the text layer (Table~\ref{tab:masked}); on print-faithful items Algebra~II does not (Table~\ref{tab:faithful}). Geometry does not beat its modal letter $0.289$ on 7B ($0.319$, $n=166$, interval $[0.253, 0.392]$) and does not clear chance on 14B ($0.301$ $[0.235, 0.373]$). EQAO grade~6 does not clear chance on 7B ($0.400$, $n=40$, interval $[0.225, 0.575]$ versus $0.255$). \paragraph{Option-numeral isomorphs.}
The probes above leave the option strings untouched, and the masked-stem reader sees them in full; released Regents forms are widely mirrored, so a memorized item could carry the rate. A pre-registered control (Appendix~\ref{app:lm}) rescores the print-faithful Algebra~I population with every numeral in the option strings replaced by a value-changing, form-preserving isomorph: same digit count, sign, decimal places, and comma grouping, the same replacement wherever a literal repeats within the item, with the masked stem and the key letter unchanged. The primary arm preserves the rank order of the options' leading numbers; a secondary arm scrambles it. The 61 Algebra~I items whose options carry no numeral are unchanged under both arms and are excluded from the paired difference. Original letters were re-scored from the same model load. Qwen 14B reproduced $592/606=0.977$ of its saved letters, below the pre-registered floor of $0.98$ (Qwen 7B $595/606=0.982$, Phi-4 $606/606=1.000$), so its perturbed arms were not scored. The faithful subset is not a random sample of any cell: in Algebra~I, 40 percent of faithful items have word options against 10 percent of not-faithful items, and administrations before 2023 dominate. It answers whether the effect exists on items whose text is what students saw; it does not estimate the rate on the full item population.

\paragraph{Which tags carry the Algebra~I row.}
Under the pre-registered verb-class rule (Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-method}), the 358 Algebra~I items split into 164 whose cluster heading names execution, 151 recognition or interpretation, and 43 mixed; Algebra~II is 120, 93, and 35. Table~\ref{tab:verbclass} gives the masked-stem rows by class on print-faithful items. On execution-tagged, print-faithful Algebra~I items ($n=75$) no model clears the channel bar: Qwen 7B $27/75=0.360$ $[0.253, 0.480]$ clears chance but not modal B $0.293$; Qwen 14B $25/75=0.333$ $[0.240, 0.440]$ clears neither; Phi-4 $29/75=0.387$ $[0.293, 0.507]$ has a lower bound equal to the modal frequency ($22/75$), not above it. Without the print filter, execution-tagged Algebra~I ($n=164$) clears on Qwen 14B only. The Algebra~I row is carried by the recognition-or-interpretation class: on those faithful items ($n=83$) all three models clear ($0.518$, $0.446$, $0.506$ against modal D $0.265$), as they do on the 26 faithful mixed items. Execution-tagged items have the lowest masked-stem rate of the three classes on every model, which is the direction one expects if the withheld givens are what those tags ask the solver to operate on. On Algebra~II no class clears on faithful items for any model.

So the Algebra~I claim stays at the cell level, on print-faithful items, with the caveat explicit: the recovered keys sit on items whose publisher's tag names recognizing or interpreting a form (interpret the structure of expressions, the function concept and notation, zeros and factors), and on those items a pass does not require the given quantities. The claim is not that a pass on execution-tagged items omits the tagged operation; that subset did not clear. The split classifies the tag's text, not each item's demand, and an item-level coding could move items between classes.

\begin{quote}
A. $92.174$\quad B. $85.043$\quad C. $79.451$\quad D. $43.256$
\end{quote}

Published key: C. File tag B1-KU: B1 is EQAO's content code on this item; KU is the knowledge-and-understanding category. Item id \texttt{eqao-2023-g6-q1}.

The sorted numbers are $43.256$, $79.451$, $85.043$, $92.174$. The rule picks index $\lfloor (4-1)/2 \rfloor = 1$, which is $79.451$, option C. The program never reads the stem and does not inspect tenths digits. Matching the key on this item does not require identifying a tenths digit.

This item is print-faithful under the check in Section~\ref{sec:print-audit}. The geometry row is the same program on 279 Part~I items, the multiple-choice session of the New York Regents geometry examination; on the 99 print-faithful items among them the rate is the same and the interval covers chance, so that row is a text-layer residue.

\subsection{What would refute these claims}

The EQAO grade~6 lower-central claim would be false if the published keys on the scored items were wrong, if the implemented rule picked any index other than $\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor$ on the sorted parsed option values, or if a recomputed lower 95\% bootstrap confidence interval on the same correct/incorrect flags on the 14 print-faithful items did not exceed chance. The post-hoc strict fidelity variant ($n=10$, $5/10$) already does not clear, and the row is billed as thin. A family-wise Holm miss is not a refutation of the per-cell bar; it is reported above. An EQAO witness under upper-central or nearest-to-mean would also change the specification claim on that row. The geometry lower-central row is already not claimed: its print-faithful interval covers chance.

The STAAR grade~5 closure discovery would be false if the published keys on those 40 fired items were wrong, if the implemented depth-2 closure with the registered tie-break did not produce 21 correct, or if the one-sided binomial $p$ against chance $0.25$ were larger than the Holm threshold on 67. That row is a discovery-set Holm reject that failed replication: the registered prediction on held-out filtered grade~5 items was a lower 95\% CI strictly above $0.250$, and the powered combined set is $21/64=0.328$, CI $[0.219, 0.438]$. Artifact numerals on 13 discovery stems and the three-administration sign test remain caveats on the discovery construct.

The Algebra~I masked-stem claim would be false if a recomputed lower 95\% interval on the print-faithful withheld-quantity items did not exceed both chance and that subset's modal-letter frequency on that scorer, or if the masking left a computable given quantity in the stem. That is the test Algebra~II failed. A Holm miss against chance in the 68-test family would remove the family-wise statement; it would not by itself remove the per-cell channel bar if the interval still cleared. Mistral failing the same bar does not refute the Qwen or Phi-4 rows. A with-stem ceiling at the masked-stem rate, as on Mistral, is a capability miss, not a missing-cue miss. The option-numeral control tests the mechanism behind the claim rather than the claim: had the rank-preserving rate on the faithful items fallen to chance, the original rate would not have been a form effect. It did not on either scored model; on Qwen 7B it fell by $0.110$ and stayed above the bar (Table~\ref{tab:isomorph}). The claim does not extend to execution-tagged items; if a finer, item-level coding of the tags moved the recognition-tagged items into an execution class, the caveat in Section~\ref{sec:print-audit} would have to be rewritten.

The Algebra~I image-backsolve existence row would be false if honest $n$ fired were below 10, if the lower 95\% interval on those 15 flags were not strictly above chance $0.25$, or if the 14 hits were invented formulas rather than displayed equations. A Holm statement about this row is not claimed.

A TIMSS knowing, applying, or reasoning witness would have been any catalog, strategy, or partial-input program that clears the bar stated for its channel, including the withheld-quantity language-model bar. No such row exists in this file. Public selected-response $n$ is 65, 54, and 68. Bypass rates below the clustered floors in Table~\ref{tab:power} would not have been seen at that $n$.
